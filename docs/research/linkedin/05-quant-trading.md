# LinkedIn Profile Rules: Quant & Trading (`quant_trading`)

Scope: UK undergraduates and masters students (1st year to final year / MSc) targeting quantitative trading, quantitative research and trading-adjacent developer roles at proprietary trading firms, market makers and systematic funds: Jane Street, Optiver, IMC, Citadel Securities, Hudson River Trading (HRT), G-Research, DRW, Flow Traders, SIG. Covers first-year programmes, insight weeks, penultimate-year internships, placements and graduate roles for the 2026-27 cycle.

Compiled: 2026-10-14. All URLs accessed 2026-10-14. Status: research input for the Placed LinkedIn scorer. Not recruiting advice.

Conventions follow `docs/research/cv-rules-finance-consulting.md` section 0 (Check = RULE / LLM / HYBRID; Severity = critical / major / minor; Evidence = A / B / C / D). Citation tags [Qx] refer to section 10. Rules in this file **override or add to** the generic LinkedIn baseline (identity, headline, About, experience, skills, Featured, recommendations, activity, CV consistency). The CV-side quant rules (QNT-01 to QNT-12 in `cv-rules-finance-consulting.md`) remain the authority on what a quant CV must contain; this file covers what changes when the same evidence appears on LinkedIn.

---

## 0. Bottom line (read this first)

1. **LinkedIn matters less for quant than for almost any other Placed sector.** None of the nine firms in scope publishes LinkedIn guidance for applicants, and none asks for a LinkedIn URL in the materials I could read. Applications go through the firm's own portal with a CV (Jane Street, Optiver, IMC, HRT) [Q1][Q3][Q4][Q8], followed by online assessments and interviews. The hiring signal comes from the CV, tests and interviews, not from the profile.
2. **What LinkedIn is for here:** (a) a clean, accurate public record that matches the CV, because interviewers and recruiters may look you up ("a potential employer will find your social media presence, anyway" [Q10]); (b) following firms for programme dates, which Cambridge's careers service explicitly advises [Q11]; (c) light networking with alumni; (d) a place to show competition results and code links that a reader can verify.
3. **Discretion is the sector-specific risk.** Trading firms treat strategy and P&L information as highly confidential [Q12], use long non-competes, and have even been reported giving them to interns [Q10 p.80]. A student profile that discloses desk P&L, strategy logic, latency figures or assessment questions is a negative signal. This is the one area where the quant scorer should be **stricter** than the baseline.
4. **Posting is not expected.** It is neutral at best. There is no evidence that posting helps quant applicants, and there is anecdotal evidence that people in the industry keep low profiles [Q15]. The scorer should give activity close to zero weight and should only flag risky posts.
5. **Evidence is thin.** Most of what follows is graded B/C/D. Employer evidence (grade A) exists for what these firms value (competitions, games, programming, quantitative ability, no finance knowledge required, honest skill levels), not for LinkedIn itself. I apply that employer evidence to the profile and say so.

## 2. What a strong student profile looks like, section by section

Input notes: LinkedIn's "Save to PDF" works only on desktop, and only for profiles in English with the member's language set to English [Q17]. From the export format I have seen (grade D: **verify against 3-5 real exports before you build on this**), the PDF holds the name, headline, location, Contact (profile URL plus listed websites), Top Skills, Languages, Certifications, Honors-Awards, Publications, Summary (About), Experience with descriptions, and Education. It does **not** hold the photo, banner, Featured, Activity/posts, or Recommendations, and probably not Projects or Volunteering. Rules that depend on those sections need a screenshot or self-report.

### 2.1 Headline
**Formula:** `[Degree] @ [University] ([year or grad year]) | [strongest verifiable quant signal: competition rank / rating / olympiad] | [2-3 core tools or areas]`. Lead with evidence, not aspiration. Quant firms say they hire on thinking ability and do not require finance knowledge [Q1][Q5], so "aspiring trader" phrases add nothing, and a concrete rank adds a lot (Optiver competitive-activity signal [Q3]; IMC Prosperity hiring outcomes [Q7]). Evidence: B (employer signal applied to the headline; the formula itself is D).

Good:
1. `Maths & Computer Science @ UCL (2028) | BMO Round 1 Distinction | IMC Prosperity 3: top 5% | Python, C++`
2. `MSci Physics, Imperial | Codeforces Expert (1,850) | Probability, statistics, ML`
3. `BSc Statistics, LSE (2nd year) | Kaggle competition bronze medal | Time series & Python`

Bad:
1. `Aspiring Quant Trader | Passionate about Financial Markets | Future Jane Streeter` (aspiration, no evidence, names a firm you are not at).
2. `Quant Trading Intern @ Optiver | Made £1.2m on Euro Stoxx options` (P&L disclosure; also implausible for an intern; discretion breach).
3. `Student at University College London` (LinkedIn default; no signal).

### 2.2 About
- **Length:** 3 to 5 short sentences, about 300 to 600 characters. IMC's preference for "clear, concise" material [Q4] is CV advice, but it fits the sector norm. Quant readers want density. Evidence: B for brevity (CV read-across), D for the exact character range.
- **Structure:** (1) What you study, plus your strongest quantitative evidence. (2) One or two projects or competitions, with a verifiable result and a link. (3) What you are looking for (e.g. "2027 quant trading / research internships"). (4) Optional: one line on how you work with others (teaching, team competitions). The firms stress collaboration: Jane Street wants "strong quantitative minds and collaborative team players" [Q1].
- **Avoid:** market-commentary clichés ("fascinated by the markets since I was 12"), a list of every language, and claims of profitable personal trading strategies with no method or link.

### 2.3 Experience (how it differs from CV bullets)
- On the CV, bullets are quantified outcomes. On LinkedIn, **quant-firm experience should be described at the level of method and scope, not results**: what you built, in which language, for which kind of desk. Leave out P&L, Sharpe ratios of live strategies, position sizes, latency figures, venue-specific logic and internal tool names. Firms treat this information as confidential, and recruitment is seen as a leak channel [Q12]. Evidence: B/C. The detail rule is D.
- Non-quant experience (tutoring, software internships, research assistantships) can carry normal quantified bullets.
- Keep 1 to 3 bullets per role. LinkedIn is not the place to repeat the whole CV.
- Pass: "Built a Python tool to analyse fill quality for the options desk; presented findings to traders at week 8." Fail: "Improved desk P&L by 14% by adjusting quoting width on Eurex index options during the open."

### 2.4 Featured
Low weight, and not visible in the PDF. Useful items: a GitHub repo for a well-documented project (backtest, paper replication, competition bot), a write-up of a competition approach (only after the competition ends and if its rules allow), a publication or dissertation. Not useful: certificates from finance e-learning, reposted market news. Evidence: C. eFC quotes a fintech recruiter on GitHub: "if I'm sending a CV over, and they have an active GitHub, I will include it" [Q10 p.118]. That is fintech, not quant, so it is only a read-across.

### 2.6 Certifications
- **Worth listing:** none is required. A relevant university-level online course in ML or statistics is neutral to slightly positive. Competition certificates belong in Honors & Awards, not Certifications.
- **Noise:** Bloomberg Market Concepts, CFA Level I "candidate" for a quant trader applicant, "Excel for Finance", Forage virtual experiences from banks (fine to list for banking; neutral here), crypto/day-trading courses. Jane Street does not test finance knowledge and says many staff had little to none [Q5] (see QNT-08). Evidence: A for "finance knowledge not required"; D for "these specific certificates are noise".

### 2.7 Education
- Degree, university, expected graduation year. List 3 to 6 quantitative modules (probability, stochastic processes, numerical methods, ML, algorithms). eFC reports Jane Street staff saying "the modules and projects you choose are the most important part of your degree" [Q13].
- **Grades:** list them if strong (First / ≥ 70% / top-x% rank). Jane Street has no GPA requirement [Q1], so a missing grade is not penalised on LinkedIn. The CV carries the grade (FCQ-04 / QNT-12). Evidence: B.
- A-levels / school: optional on LinkedIn. Include a school-level olympiad result under Honors & Awards, not under school.
- Societies: a committee role in a trading, maths, quant or competitive-programming society is a positive signal. eFC's intern analysis: "Many interns served as executives of university societies like the Carnegie Mellon Competitive Programming club" [Q14] (US-centric sample).

### 2.8 Honors & Awards (the most important section for quant)
- List olympiad results (UKMT BMO/BPhO/BIO, IMO/EGMO), competitive programming ratings or placings (Codeforces, ICPC, Advent of Code leaderboard), trading competitions (IMC Prosperity, Citadel datathons, university trading challenges), Kaggle medals, scholarships and prizes. **Always give the rank, percentile or medal and the size of the field.** "Participant" is weak, but honest.
- Employer evidence: Optiver's competitive-activities criterion [Q3]; IMC hiring from Prosperity [Q7]; eFC's Jane Street intern analysis cites a "top 3%" CodinGame result and olympiad champions [Q14]. Evidence: A (employer value) / B.

### 2.9 Activities and interests
Chess (with rating), bridge, poker, Go, competitive programming, puzzle hunts and team sports at a stated level all match Optiver's "strategic games" signal [Q3]. Frame poker as strategy and probability practice, not as winnings or gambling (D). Interests go in About or Honors on LinkedIn, because there is no Interests section that shows in the PDF.

### 2.10 Recommendations
Low weight. Not in the PDF. A recommendation from a research supervisor or a competition coach is mildly positive. Do not ask a trading-firm mentor to describe your work on the desk (discretion). Evidence: D.

### 2.11 Activity / posting norms
- **Neutral to risky.** There is no evidence that posting helps quant applicants. Anonymous industry discussion describes quant/HFT staff minimising or removing profiles to avoid phishing and because of proprietary-knowledge concerns [Q15] (grade C-, anonymous forum, 2022). Jane Street warns about impersonators using social media and DMs [Q16].
- **Risky posts:** online-assessment or interview questions; "my internship day" posts showing screens, desks or internal tools; P&L or strategy claims; "I got an offer from X" posts that disclose offer terms. Reasonable posts: a competition result after it has been announced; a technical write-up of a public project.
- The scorer should **not** penalise zero activity for this sector.

### 2.12 Photo and banner
- Photo: a plain, professional headshot is normal. It is not visible in the PDF export. **"Profiles with photos get 21x more views" is unverified:** it is widely repeated and attributed to a LinkedIn marketing post, but the 2017 LinkedIn blog URL that search results point to now redirects to a generic blog index, and I could not find the primary data. Do not show it in the UI as a fact.
- Banner: irrelevant to quant screening. A neutral banner, or one showing the university, is fine. Do not put firm logos you do not work for in the banner. Do not use the #OpenToWork frame (see LI-QUANT_TRADING-13).

### 2.13 Open to Work
LinkedIn's own help page says that even with "Recruiters only" visibility it "can't guarantee complete privacy" [Q18]. For students this matters little. Recruiters-only is still the sensible default, and the public green frame adds nothing in a sector that does not source this way (D).

## 3. Sector-specific red flags and myths

**Red flags (profile content):**
1. Disclosing P&L, strategy logic, positions, latency or venue specifics from a trading internship or spring week [Q12][Q10 p.80].
2. Publishing online-assessment, HireVue or interview questions, or "how I passed X's OA" posts. No in-scope firm statement on this was fetched (D), but it is a reasonable inference from the sector's confidentiality culture [Q12].
3. Claiming profitable personal algorithmic trading ("my bot made 300% this year") with no method, no out-of-sample test and no link. This invites hard questioning and reads as naive (D; consistent with QNT-05 honest-results guidance).
4. Inflated skill levels: "Expert in C++" from one module. IMC: rate yourself against professionals [Q4].
5. Finance-heavy branding with little quantitative evidence ("Passionate about markets | CFA L1 | Crypto"). Finance knowledge is not required [Q1][Q5]; this does not hurt on its own but displaces the signals that do matter.
6. Inconsistency with the CV, e.g. a competition "top 1%" on LinkedIn and "top 10%" on the CV, or different internship dates.
7. Listing a firm you attended for a one-day event as an "internship" or "Trader" role. Use Honors/Activities or "Insight programme participant", with accurate dates.
8. Responding to "recruiters" for these firms over WhatsApp, Telegram or LinkedIn DMs that move off-platform. Jane Street says its recruiter emails always end @janestreet.com [Q16]. This is safety advice, not a score item.

**Myths:**
- *"Quant firms headhunt students on LinkedIn, so optimise for search."* Unsupported. The firms' documented student funnels are their own programmes and competitions [Q5][Q6][Q7][Q8].
- *"You need to post regularly to be visible."* No evidence for this sector; see 2.11.
- *"Finance certifications make you stand out for trading."* Contradicted by the firms' own statements [Q1][Q5].
- *"21x more views with a photo."* Unverified primary source (see 2.12).
- *"A secretive industry means you should hide your profile entirely."* Overcorrection. Students who land these internships have public profiles listing firms, societies and competitions [Q13][Q14]. Discretion is about content, not existence.
- *"Your university decides everything."* eFC's own data shows a spread of universities, and Jane Street hires "from many different universities" [Q1][Q13]. Do not encode university prestige into the LinkedIn score.
