# 01 — The early-careers market 2025–2026 and what it means for the CV

Researcher 1 of 6 · compiled 2026-10-13 · last re-verified 2026-10-18 against `docs/research/audit-2026-10-18/10-fit-ats-market.md` · scope: UK first, with US/EU for comparison.

**What this adds.** `cv-rules-general.md` §1 already covers these: 140 applications per vacancy (now 141, ISE 2026; see §A0), the 2:1 threshold trend, 73%/46% student AI use, and "employer AI use is minimal". This file does not repeat them except as context. It adds:

- the demand-side collapse
- the application-volume and AI arms race
- the new economics evidence that AI has devalued written application signals
- how named employers now run their funnels, and where the CV actually sits in them
- feature implications

**Evidence grades.**

- **A**: primary data or peer-reviewed / NBER / official statistics
- **B**: reputable institutional survey (ISE, NACE, Prospects/Jisc, Handshake research, Indeed Hiring Lab), or an employer's own careers page
- **C**: reputable press, or a vendor survey
- **D**: our inference

## Key findings

### A0. 2026 update (added 2026-10-18)

The ISE Student Recruitment Report 2026 came out on 14 Oct 2026, after this file was first compiled. It supersedes the 2025 headline figures used below. Where a 2025 figure is kept, it is labelled by year to show the trend.

- **Sample:** 155 employers, more than 1.7M applications, 31,577 hires (2025/26 cycle).
- **Volume:** **141** applications per graduate vacancy (140 in 2025). London 172; financial and professional services 158; apprenticeships 96.
- **Hiring:** graduate hiring **+4%** in 2025/26, after −8% in 2024/25. Employers forecast **+7%** for 2026/27. By employer, **45%** increased intake, **15%** held it and **40%** cut it.
- **Misconduct:** **92%** suspected or confirmed misconduct (85% in 2025, 78% in 2024).
- **Employer AI use:** **16%** currently use AI in recruitment, 29% plan to within a year, 52% don't plan to.
- **Candidate AI policy:** **82%** give candidates AI guidance (55% in 2025), so about 18% give none. **Up to 64%** accept candidate AI use (46% in 2025), usually for CVs and applications, not tests or interviews.
- **Redesign:** **77%** are reviewing or redesigning selection (79% in 2025).
- **Volume tactics:** "automated eligibility screening", shorter application windows and "limiting roles or programmes per candidate".
- Internship and placement ratios (101 / 123 per hire, ISE 2025) are not in the 2026 summary article; the full report PDF is needed to update them.
- Source: ISE, *Graduate jobs stabilise, but AI use disrupts recruitment* (Student Recruitment Report 2026), 14 Oct 2026, https://ise.org.uk/knowledge/insights/615/graduate_jobs_stabilise_but_ai_use_disrupts_recruitment (accessed 2026-10-18) — **B**

**Graduate route shortens from January 2027.** Bachelor's and master's Graduate-route applications made on or after 1 Jan 2027 get **18 months** instead of 2 years; PhD stays at 3 years. This affects 2026–27 international finalists applying to 2027 graduate schemes: their post-study window before needing sponsorship is shorter.
- Home Office Statement of Changes, 14 Oct 2025, via The PIE News, https://thepienews.com/shorter-graduate-route-from-january-2027-uk-govt-confirms/ (accessed 2026-10-18) — **B**; UKCISA summary — **B**

### A. Demand: fewer graduate jobs, especially in professional services

**F1. UK graduate hiring shrank to 2025, unevenly, then stabilised in 2025/26 (ISE).**
- **ISE members (2025):** graduate hiring fell **8%** in 2024/25, the weakest year since 2020. Employers forecast a further **7%** cut for 2025/26. School/college-leaver hiring rose **8%**, and the graduate:apprentice ratio fell from 2.3:1 to **1.8:1** (forecast 1.6:1). By employer, **42%** cut graduate hiring; the 25% flat / 33% increased split is not on the ISE page ⚠.
  - ISE, *5 trends from ISE's Recruitment Survey 2025*, 22 Oct 2025, https://ise.org.uk/knowledge/insights/498/5_trends_you_need_to_know_from_ises_recruitment_survey_2025/ — **B**
  - C. Tyler (ISE), Wonkhe, 15 Oct 2025, https://wonkhe.com/blogs/graduate-jobs-and-recruitment-reality/ — **B**
- **ISE members (2026 update):** the forecast cut did not happen. Graduate hiring rose **+4%** in 2025/26, with **+7%** forecast for 2026/27; 45% increased intake, 15% held, 40% cut (ISE 2026, §A0) — **B**
- **High Fliers (UK top-100 employers):** vacancies fell 6.4% (2023), 14.6% (2024) and 5.1% (2025). That is **−24.5% cumulatively since 2022**, worse than the 2020 pandemic drop (−12.3%). A further −0.5% is forecast for 2026.
  - High Fliers, *The Graduate Market in 2026* (published Jan 2026), https://highfliers.co.uk/publication-the-graduate-market-report — **B**
- **Sector split.** Accounting and professional services are down about a third versus 2022/23. Investment banks plan **2,365** UK graduate hires in 2026, up from 2,069 in 2025.
  - High Fliers via eFinancialCareers, https://www.efinancialcareers.com/news/investment-banks-may-hire-record-graduates-in-2026-getting-a-job-could-be-harder-than-ever — **C** (press relaying B data; article date not shown)
- **Big 4 intakes, reported by press:** KPMG about −29% (1,399 → 942), Deloitte −18%, EY −11%, PwC −6%. ⚠ Unverifiable: the primary (Times / City AM) was not reached in the 2026-10-18 re-check. Keep out of UI copy.
  - Accountancy Today, 24 Jun 2025, https://www.accountancytoday.co.uk/2025/06/24/big-four-firms-cut-grad-jobs-in-favour-of-ai/ — **C ⚠** (403 on fetch; figures from snippet; original source likely The Times / City AM)

**F2. Job-board data shows the same squeeze.**
- **Indeed UK:** recent-graduate postings were down **33% year on year** in mid-June 2025, the toughest market since 2018.
  - Reported via HR Grapevine / UKTN, Jun 2025, https://www.uktech.news/ai/ai-drives-sharp-drop-in-graduate-roles-research-finds-20250625 — **C ⚠** (press only; not verified 2026-10-18; keep out of UI)
- **Indeed mid-2026 update:** graduate postings down **7%** YoY at their lowest since 2020. Overall UK postings were **32% below** the Feb 2020 baseline. AI was mentioned in **9.4%** of all UK postings, and in nearly **50%** of data/analytics postings.
  - Indeed Hiring Lab UK, *2026 Mid-Year UK Jobs & Hiring Trends Report*, 3 Aug 2026, https://hiringlab.indeed.com/uk/blog/2026/08/03/mid-year-uk-jobs-hiring-trends-report/ — **B** (primary verified 2026-10-18; data/analytics share 48.8%)
  - Workplace Journal summarising Indeed, 3 Aug 2026, https://workplacejournal.co.uk/2026/08/graduate-job-postings-at-lowest-level-since-pandemic-indeed/ — **C** (relays B data)
- **Adzuna:** entry-level vacancies (graduate, apprentice, intern, junior) are down **32%** since ChatGPT launched (Nov 2022). They are now about 25% of all ads, down from about 30%.
  - IT Brief UK, Jun 2025, https://itbrief.co.uk/story/uk-entry-level-tech-jobs-drop-by-32-amid-chatgpt-rise — **C ⚠** (press only; not verified 2026-10-18; keep out of UI)

**F3. Outcomes are cooling, not collapsing.** These official statistics put the doom headlines in proportion.
- **HESA Graduate Outcomes 2022/23** (pub. 17 Jul 2025): **59%** in full-time employment at 15 months.
  - https://www.graduateoutcomes.ac.uk/news-and-blogs/88-of-202223-graduates-in-work-or-further-study-according-to-new-hesa-official-statistics — **A**
- **2023/24 cohort:**
  - 81% in work and 7% unemployed
  - median salary £30k, up from £28.5k
  - Jisc's Charlie Ball calls it "a cooling not a collapse"
  - Source: THE, 4 Jun 2026, https://www.timeshighereducation.com/news/drop-graduate-employment-cooling-not-collapse — **A** data via **C** report
- **US comparison:** unemployment among bachelor's holders aged 20–24 was 9.5% (Sept 2025). NACE's spring update projects +5.6% hiring for the Class of 2026, up from a +1.6% autumn forecast.
  - NACE, https://naceweb.org/job-market/trends-and-predictions/hiring-flat-for-the-college-class-of-2026 — **B**

**F4. Is AI the cause? The evidence is real but not settled.**
- **US payroll data (ADP)** shows workers aged 22–25 in the most AI-exposed occupations had a **13% relative employment decline**, controlling for firm shocks. Older workers in the same jobs and less-exposed jobs held steady. The effect is concentrated where AI *automates* rather than *augments*.
  - Brynjolfsson, Chandar & Chen, *Canaries in the Coal Mine?*, Stanford Digital Economy Lab working paper, rev. Nov 2025, https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/app/uploads/2025/11/CanariesintheCoalMine_Nov25.pdf — **A** (working paper)
- **Handshake** concludes AI "is not yet a clear explanation". It points instead to 2021–22 overhiring, rate rises and US federal hiring freezes.
  - Handshake, *What 2025 taught us about the labor market*, Jan 2026, https://joinhandshake.com/research/economic-research/labor-market-retro-january-2026/ — **B ⚠**
- **D:** treat AI as one driver among several. It hits routine entry-level professional-services work hardest, which is consistent with the Big 4 cuts.

### B. Supply: application volume and the AI arms race

**F5. Volume per opening keeps rising, and internships are now as competitive as graduate jobs.**
- **ISE 2024/25:** 1.8M applications for about 31,000 hires across 155 employers, about **1.7% success per application**. Applications per graduate vacancy grew 38 (2002/3) → 86 (2022/3) → **140** (2023/24 and 2024/25) → **141** (2025/26, ISE 2026; London 172, financial and professional services 158). Retail/FMCG/tourism is about 290 (2025).
  - ISE Top 10 stats of 2025, 8 Dec 2025, https://ise.org.uk/knowledge/insights/513/ise_top_10_stats_of_2025_you_need_to_know/ — **B**
- **Internships:** **101 applications per hire**, up from 84. **Placements:** **123 per hire**, up from 87. Internship applications rose **34%** among 52 employers, placement applications **17%**. The median internship vacancy drew 63 applications (+29%). 43% of internships/placements are in London.
  - ISE, *5 internship and placement trends to watch in 2026*, 24 Nov 2025, https://ise.org.uk/knowledge/insights/508/5_internship_and_placement_trends_to_watch_in_2026/ — **B**
  - Note: ISE's "+16% / +27%" figures are the changes in the **applications-per-hire ratio** for matched employers (internships 87→101; placements 97→123), not rises in applications (ISE 508/513). They are consistent with the per-hire figures above.
- **US (Handshake):** the Class of 2026 submits **23 applications per full-time job**, up from 20.8 a year earlier and **double** the Class of 2023 (11). Full-time early-talent postings are down 15% YoY. 62% of seniors are pessimistic about their prospects.
  - Handshake, *Class of 2026* report, https://joinhandshake.com/themes/handshake/dist/assets/downloads/network-trends/[HNT%2026]%20Class%20of%202026.pdf — **B ⚠**
  - https://joinhandshake.com/research/economic-research/class-of-2026-ai-outlook/ (Apr 2026) — **B**
- **LinkedIn:** about **11,000 applications per minute**, +45% YoY. Recruiters report near-identical CVs.
  - Semafor citing NYT, 24 Jun 2025, https://www.semafor.com/article/06/24/2025/recruiters-swamped-with-ai-generated-job-applications — **C**

**F6. AI use and high-volume "spray" applying go together.**
- **Prospects at Jisc Early Careers Survey** (n≈5,000 recent graduates, Jun 2026):
  - **20% applied for 100+ jobs** (attributed to this Jisc survey, not HESA, by THE, 4 Jun 2026, https://www.timeshighereducation.com/news/drop-graduate-employment-cooling-not-collapse — C relaying B)
  - students submitting **100+ applications** rose from **8% → 13%**
  - **66%** of those applying to 101–200 roles used AI, versus **40%** of those submitting ≤20
  - **44%** feel AI threatens their prospects
  - only **38%** think AI CV screening is acceptable
  - students who got careers guidance on AI were over twice as likely to see AI as increasing their opportunities (27% vs 12%)
  - Source: https://luminate.prospects.ac.uk/1-in-5-graduates-apply-for-more-than-100-jobs — **B**
- **Bright Network**, *What do graduates want? 2026/27* (n>10,000): **53%** use AI to enhance applications, and **25% would use it even if the employer told them not to**.
  - https://employers.brightnetwork.co.uk/research/what-do-graduates-want-2025-26-duplicate/ — **B** (landing page; full report gated)
- **Handshake:** **85%** of US Class of 2026 seniors use AI (about 50% for the Class of 2024). 58% say they need stronger AI skills, but only 28% say their course meaningfully integrated AI. — **B**

**F7. Economics evidence: LLMs have devalued the polished, tailored written application as a signal.** This is the most important new finding for a CV product.
- **Pre-LLM field experiment (≈480k jobseekers):** algorithmic writing assistance on résumés raised hires by **8%** and wages by **10%** (published version), with no fall in employer satisfaction. Clearer writing helped employers read ability.
  - Wiles, Munyikwa & Horton, *Management Science* 71(12), 2025 (NBER w30886), https://www.nber.org/papers/w30886 — **A**
- **Post-LLM (Freelancer.com):** before LLMs, employers paid a premium for applications *customised* to the posting. After LLMs, that premium **disappeared**. In the structural counterfactual, top-quintile workers are hired **19% less** often and bottom-quintile **14% more**, so the market becomes "significantly less meritocratic".
  - Galdin & Silbert, *Making Talk Cheap: Generative AI and Labor Market Signaling*, arXiv 2511.08785, 11 Nov 2025, https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.08785 — **A** (working paper)
- **D (synthesis):** polish and tailoring are now **table stakes, not a differentiator**. What still separates candidates is information AI cannot cheaply fake:
  - verifiable specifics (named projects, links, numbers with context)
  - real experience
  - performance on proctored tests and interviews

  A CV tool that only increases keyword match is optimising a signal that the market has already discounted.

### C. Employer response

**F8. Employers are redesigning processes, not banning AI.**
- **ISE SRS 2026 (current):**
  - **77%** are reviewing or redesigning selection (79% in 2025)
  - **up to 64%** accept candidate AI use (46% in 2025), usually for CVs and applications, not tests or interviews
  - **82%** give candidates AI guidance (55% in 2025)
  - volume tactics include automated eligibility screening, shorter windows and limiting roles or programmes per candidate
  - Source: ISE 2026 (§A0) — **B**
- **ISE SRS 2025 (trend baseline):**
  - **79%** were redesigning (**33%**, up from 23%) or reviewing (**46%**) selection because of GenAI
  - only **10%** banned candidate AI
  - about **46–50%** allowed it, mostly "for drafting covering letters and CVs and completing online application questions"
  - Sources: https://ise.org.uk/knowledge/insights/498/... (above); ISE, *How are employers using AI in early careers recruitment*, 12 Nov 2025, https://ise.org.uk/knowledge/insights/505/how_are_employers_using_ai_in_early_careers_recruitment — **B**
- **Cheating is the real fight:**
  - **92%** of employers suspected or confirmed misconduct in 2025/26 (ISE 2026), up from **85%** in 2025 and 78% in 2024
  - "frequent" cheating doubled from **7% → 15%** (2025)
  - **61%** cite undisclosed AI use *during interviews* as the most common misconduct (2025)
  - about **1 in 10** students admit using AI in online tests or interviews
  - **30%** of employers have no detection approach
  - Source: S. Isherwood (ISE), Prospects Luminate, Oct 2025, https://luminate.prospects.ac.uk/early-careers-recruitment-in-2025-integrity-inclusion-entry-standards — **B**
- **Authenticity worry is rising.** **67%** of employers now worry that graduates misrepresent their abilities using AI in selection. The equivalent 2025 figure was 48%. (⚠ the 67% was not re-opened in the 2026-10-18 re-check.)
  - ISE, *5 top trends from ISE's Development Survey 2026*, 18 May 2026, https://ise.org.uk/knowledge/insights/565/5_top_trends_from_ises_development_survey_2026/ — **B**
- **US comparison:** 28% of candidates admit generating fake work samples with AI, and 47% admit embellishing qualifications.
  - Greenhouse *2025 AI in Hiring* report (n≈4,100 across US/UK/IE/DE), via Unleash, 15 Jul 2025, https://unleash.ai/emerging-tech/news/greenhouse-28-of-candidates-now-use-ai-to-generate-fake-work-samples-and-47-embellish-qualifications — **C ⚠** (ATS vendor survey)

**F9. Early-careers employers automate *tests*, not CV judgement, so far.**
- **ISE 2025 (fully automated shares):**
  - numerical reasoning **57%**
  - situational judgement **53%**
  - verbal **49%**
  - game-based AI-only **15%**
- **Human-plus-system hybrids:** strengths tests 42%, job simulations 33%.
- **Attitudes:** **71%** prefer human-centric approaches, but **62%** expect to use AI in recruitment within five years and **70%** expect more automation.
  - ISE insight 505 (above) — **B**
- **Conflicting evidence:** general UK HR vendor surveys claim much higher AI screening, e.g. "62% of HR teams use AI to screen" (Zinc) and "73% of recruiters use AI". These cover all hiring, not graduate schemes, and come from vendors.
  - Resultsense / TNW round-ups, 2025–26 — **C ⚠**
- **D:** for large UK graduate schemes, assume humans or rules read the form/CV, and machines score the tests. For high-volume SME and agency roles, assume more keyword/AI ranking.

### D. What employers say they want now

**F11. Skills-based hiring is growing, but more in rhetoric than in outcomes.**
- **NACE (US):**
  - **70%** of employers use skills-based hiring for entry level, up from 65%
  - they use it most in **interviewing (87%)** and **screening (65%)**
  - GPA screening fell from **73% (2019) to 42%**
  - where GPA is ignored, the top factors are major (about 94%), industry experience (about 81%) and internships (about 79%)
  - fewer than 40% of seniors understand the term
  - Sources: NACE press, 2026, https://naceweb.org/about-us/press/2026/skills-based-hiring-grows-but-college-students-dont-fully-understand-it — **B**; percentages for the top factors via secondary summary ⚠
- **Reality check:** across 11,300 roles, dropping degree requirements raised non-graduate hiring by only **3.5 percentage points**. About **45%** of firms changed "in name only" and about one-fifth backslid.
  - Burning Glass Institute & HBS, *Skills-Based Hiring: The Long Road from Pronouncements to Practice*, Feb 2024, https://burningglassinstitute.org/research/skills-based-hiring-2024 — **A/B**
- **LinkedIn:** a skills-first approach could expand talent pools **6.1×** (8.2× for AI roles).
  - LinkedIn Economic Graph, Mar 2025, https://economicgraph.linkedin.com/content/dam/me/economicgraph/en-us/PDF/skills-based-hiring-march-2025.pdf — **B** (model-based)

**F12. The UK skill gaps employers report are behavioural, not technical. Employers will build AI literacy rather than hire for it.**
- **ISE Development Survey 2026 (n≈144):**
  - **54%** of graduates fell short on **self-awareness**
  - **46%** fell short on **resilience**
  - **35%** fell short on adapting to workplace demands
  - critical thinking and judgement are rising in importance
  - employers are "twice as likely to develop digital literacy than recruit for it"
  - 87% expect AI to *reshape* (not remove) entry-level roles
  - Sources: ISE insight 565 (above); ISE Top-10 stats — **B**
- **Bright Network 2026/27:** students think employers prioritise experience, but **employers rank experience last** among the attributes surveyed. — **B ⚠** (full instrument not seen)
- **Experience still pays off downstream.** **77%** of ISE employers say former interns are more work-ready (ISE Top-10 — **B**). Historically about half of interns/placement students convert to graduate hires in the same firm (ISE 2022 — **B ⚠**).
- **D (reconciliation):** for *screening*, UK graduate employers say they weigh potential and behaviours over prior experience. Experience matters as *evidence* of those behaviours and as a pipeline (intern → graduate offer). A CV should therefore frame experience as evidence of judgement, adaptability and self-awareness, not list duties.

**F13. AI skills in postings: concentrated by sector.**
- **9.4%** of all UK postings mention AI, but nearly **50%** of data/analytics postings do (Indeed, F2).
- In the US, **>10%** of internship postings and **4.2%** of full-time postings mention AI keywords; the full-time share nearly doubled YoY (Handshake Apr 2026 — **B**).
- **D:** "AI literacy" belongs on the CV when the target role or sector asks for it (data, tech, product, increasingly consulting). Elsewhere it should not be a scored requirement.

### E. Where the CV matters in the 2026 funnel (synthesis, D)

| Funnel stage | What is assessed | Does the CV matter? |
|---|---|---|
| 0. Eligibility / knockout (right to work, grades, location) | Form fields | Indirectly: the data must be consistent with the CV (PwC verifies grades at screening) |
| 1. Initial screen | **CV** (IB, MBB, quant, tech, SMEs) **or form** (Big 4, law, Civil Service) | **High** in CV-gated sectors; **low** in form-gated ones |
| 2. Online tests / game-based / job simulation | Automated (57% of numerical tests fully automated) | None |
| 3. Video interview (HireVue etc.) | Behaviours, motivation; AI misuse policed here (61% cite undisclosed AI in interviews as the most common misconduct, ISE 2025) | **Indirect but critical**: the interviewer probes CV claims, so every bullet must be defensible live |
| 4. Assessment centre / Superday | Case, group, technical | Medium: the CV is the discussion script |
| 5. Pre-employment screening | Grades/references verified | Any inflation is caught here (PwC: "we take any discrepancies very seriously") |

**Implication (D):** in 2026 the CV is a *gate plus a script*. It has to pass a human/keyword gate in CV-gated sectors, and then survive interrogation in interviews and verification. With AI-polished CVs now common, the bar rises for *specificity and defensibility*, not for polish.

## Myths vs evidence

| # | Myth | Verdict | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| MK1 | **"Apply to more jobs and your odds go up."** | **Weak / counterproductive at top schemes.** Heavy appliers lean on AI (66% vs 40%), which produces generic applications. Some schemes limit applications per candidate (Big 4 "one per intake year" ⚠ unverified; ISE 2026 reports limiting roles or programmes per candidate as a trend), so a weak shot can burn the cycle. Post-LLM, generic customisation earns no premium. | Prospects/Jisc 2026 (B); ISE 2026 (B); Galdin & Silbert 2025 (A) |

## Implications for Placed CV scoring & tailoring features

Each idea is tied to the findings above. Where possible these are additive to the spec (§6–§8), not rewrites.

1. **"Does your CV even matter here?" — a per-employer funnel chip** (F9, F10, §E).
   - Add an `employer_process` field to jobs: `cv_gate: high|medium|low`, stages[], `ai_policy` text and URL, and `limit_per_cycle` (only where the employer's page states it; the Big 4 "one per year" claim is ⚠ unverified).
   - On the job card and tailoring screen, show e.g. "PwC: form + tests first; CV not requested → prep your form answers and tests".
   - This extends the spec's Big 4 caveat to law and the Civil Service, and makes it data-driven.
   - Seed it for about 10 demo employers from the F10 table.

2. **A "Defensibility" check in place of more polish** (F7, F8, §E).
   - Add an LLM rule, e.g. `G-38 interview-defensible`: each bullet must contain at least one *specific, checkable anchor*, such as a named tool/project/org, a number with a baseline, or a link.
   - Flag bullets that are fluent but interchangeable as "AI-generic: an interviewer will probe this".
   - After tailoring, generate 3 "be ready to defend" interview questions from the most-changed bullets.
   - This directly targets the 67% misrepresentation concern and interview misuse (the most common form of misconduct, cited by 61% in ISE 2025), and sells well in a pitch: "we make your CV *harder to fake*, not just shinier".

3. **Reframe the tailoring success metric away from keyword match alone** (F7, MK2).
   - Galdin & Silbert show customisation lost its value once everyone could do it cheaply.
   - Keep "keyword coverage 45% → 81%", but pair it with an **evidence-density** metric: the share of JD requirements backed by a concrete anchor from the original CV.
   - Rename the headline from "match %" to "**requirements you can prove**".
   - The existing `verifyTailoring()` already guarantees no invention; surface that as "**AI-assisted, human-verifiable**". That wording matches Deloitte's and the Civil Service's "refine, don't invent" policies.

4. **Quality-over-volume nudges tied to gamification** (F5, F6, MK1).
   - The spec already gives XP for quality over volume. Add an "**application budget**" warning when a user tailors more than N applications per day.
   - For employers with a verified per-cycle limit, show a **high-stakes gate**, e.g. "This employer limits how many roles you can apply for. Your CV score for this sector is 58; reach 70 first." Do not seed PwC or Deloitte with a limit until the rule is quoted from their own pages (⚠).
   - Cite the Prospects data that heavy appliers lean on AI.

5. **Employer AI-policy chip plus integrity coaching** (F8, F10, MK3).
   - Show the employer's actual policy next to the "Tailor with AI" button, e.g. "Deloitte UK: AI OK for preparing; **banned in assessments and interviews**".
   - About 18% of employers still give no AI guidance (ISE 2026; 82% now do), and students with AI guidance were twice as optimistic. Placed can fill the remaining guidance gap ethically.

6. **Sector-conditional "AI literacy" rule** (F13, MK9).
   - Do not add a universal "mention AI skills" rule.
   - For data, SWE, product and (soft) consulting archetypes, add a `minor` relevance rule: "evidence of applied AI/tool use (what you built or automated, with outcome)".
   - For other sectors, leave it unscored.

7. **Behaviour-evidence rule for UK graduate schemes** (F12, MK8).
   - Add an LLM rule that checks the CV shows **self-awareness, adaptability/resilience, judgement** through outcomes, e.g. "changed approach after X", "learned Y from failure Z".
   - Weight it `major` for Big 4, consulting and the Civil Service. These are the gaps ISE employers report (54% self-awareness, 46% resilience).
   - Frame experience bullets as *evidence of a behaviour*, not a duty list.

8. **Application-form answer mode for form-gated employers** (F10).
   - In form-led sectors the "CV" that matters is the set of motivational answers.
   - Reuse the tailoring and verification pipeline to draft form answers, with the same source-line tracing and placeholders for missing facts.
   - Keep the employer's rules in force: if the employer bans AI-generated form answers, the button is disabled and only feedback mode is allowed.

9. **Calibration copy and myth cards** (F1–F5, myths table).
   - Add MK1–MK10 to the existing Myth-vs-evidence card set.
   - Onboarding line, for example: "About 141 applications per graduate job (ISE 2026), 101 per internship hire (ISE 2025). Placed helps you send fewer, provable applications."
   - Use only A/B-graded numbers in UI copy. Keep C/⚠ figures out of the product.
