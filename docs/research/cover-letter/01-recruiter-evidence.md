# 01: Recruiter & hiring-manager evidence

**Question:** Do cover letters get read, and what actually moves the decision?
**Scope:** UK student roles (internships, spring weeks, grad schemes), with US and platform evidence where UK data doesn't exist.
**Researched:** 2026-10-17.

## Evidence-strength legend

- **STRONG**: peer-reviewed or preprint field/lab experiment with real recruiters or real hiring outcomes, documented methods, no vendor conflict.
- **MODERATE**: neutral-body survey (SHRM, ISE), a documented vendor field experiment, or a consistent pattern across official careers services and employer process docs.
- **WEAK-vendor**: self-reported survey run by a company that sells CV or cover-letter products (ResumeGo, Zety, Resume Genius / CV Genius, iHire, TopResume). These usually give no sampling frame, question wording or margin of error, and the headline numbers get distorted as blogs repeat them. Treat them as directional only.

---

## 1. Key findings

### A. Do letters get read at all?

- **Letters get a skim at first screen, not a read.** In a 2024 ResumeGo survey of 418 US hiring professionals, 66% said they spend under 30 seconds on a cover letter at initial screening and 91% under a minute. They said they spend less time on the letter than on the CV. *WEAK-vendor* ([ResumeGo 2024](https://resumego.net/research/how-much-time-spend-resume)). No eye-tracking study measures cover letters specifically. The well-known 6s / 7.4s figures (TheLadders 2012/2018) measure **résumés**, so don't transfer them to letters.
- **Most hiring managers say they read letters, but that is self-reported.** The figures: 87% (ResumeGo, n=236, 2019–20), 83% (Zety, n=753), 83% even when optional (Resume Genius, n=625 US, 2023), and 76.5% "always read if included" (iHire, n=603, Dec 2020). *WEAK-vendor* ([ResumeGo](https://www.resumego.net/research/cover-letters/), [TechRSeries on Zety](https://techrseries.com/recruitment-and-on-boarding/zety-report-finds-81-of-recruiters-have-rejected-a-candidate-based-on-details-in-their-cover-letter/), [NYSSCPA citing Resume Genius](https://www.nysscpa.org/article-content/why-cover-letters-still-count-even-when-they-re-optional-032725), [HR Daily Advisor on iHire](https://hrdailyadvisor.com/?p=73217)).
- **Neutral and recruiting-platform sources show much lower importance.** Jobvite Recruiter Nation: 37% of recruiters said cover letters matter (2015), about 26% (2017), and 27% "consider" them (2020, n=806). SHRM (2014, n≈400 HR professionals): just over 40% rated letters important or very important. *MODERATE* ([The Muse on Jobvite 2020](https://www.themuse.com/advice/do-i-need-cover-letter), [Business Insider 2017](https://businessinsider.nl/why-cover-letters-are-useless-2017-9/), [Adweek on Jobvite 2015](https://www.adweek.com/?p=1259016), [SHRM via textbook](https://digfir-published.macmillanusa.com/PS2e/ps2e_ch10_5.html)). **The gap is consistent: surveys from vendors that sell letters report 80–90% readership, and neutral or ATS sources report 25–40% "important".**
- **One UK/IE survey exists.** CV Genius (n=625 UK and Irish hiring managers, published July 2024) found 57% call letters "influential" or "extremely influential", and 49% expect one. 59–69% prefer short over long. *WEAK-vendor* ([CV Genius 2024](https://cvgenius.com/blog/career-advice/how-important-is-a-cover-letter)). The often-quoted "85% say vital" is in the same article, but its chart segments don't support it, so don't cite it.

### B. When are letters read? (screening vs tie-break vs interview)

- **Many large UK graduate employers have replaced the letter with form questions.** PwC UK's grad application is a single online form: "You don't need to upload a CV or covering letter" ([TARGETjobs, Apr 2025](https://targetjobs.co.uk/careers-advice/cvs-applications-and-tests/pwc-graduate-application-process-explained)). UK careers services say that "Why this organisation?" form questions do the same job as a cover letter ([QMUL 2026](https://www.qmul.ac.uk/sef/news/2026/items/getting-your-application-right-what-uk-employers-expect.html)). *MODERATE*
- **Banks still ask for letters for spring weeks and internships, sometimes in a non-standard format.** eFinancialCareers says most banks require a CV and cover letter for spring weeks, and that Goldman's 2026 letter is "specially formatted", closer to a personal statement ([eFC spring weeks](https://www.efinancialcareers.com/news/spring-week-bank), [eFC Goldman 2026](https://www.efinancialcareers.com/news/goldman-2026-internships)). *MODERATE*
- **Recruiters have long preferred structured questions to free-form letters.** iHire's press release says 65.3% would rather use pre-screening questions than letters ([HR Daily Advisor](https://hrdailyadvisor.com/?p=73217)). *WEAK-vendor*. The UK grad-scheme move to forms points the same way.
- **The letter seems to work as a tie-break and as context.** In the CV Genius UK survey, 49% would consider an under-qualified applicant who sent a highly persuasive letter. In the Zety survey, the letter was recruiters' first choice of place to explain an employment gap (57%), ahead of the interview or CV. *WEAK-vendor* ([CV Genius](https://cvgenius.com/blog/career-advice/how-important-is-a-cover-letter), [TechRSeries on Zety](https://techrseries.com/recruitment-and-on-boarding/zety-report-finds-81-of-recruiters-have-rejected-a-candidate-based-on-details-in-their-cover-letter/)).
- **Gap:** no rigorous source measures how often interviewers reread the letter to prepare questions. Careers services assume they do. Treat it as plausible but unmeasured.

### C. Optional letters: send one?

- **Sending one is a small positive and rarely a negative.** In ResumeGo's field experiment (7,287 fictitious applications to real US job-board postings, Jul 2019–Jan 2020), callbacks were about 10.7% with no letter, 12.5% with a generic letter and 16.4% with a tailored one. Tailored vs no letter is the widely quoted "+53%". Tailored vs generic is about +31%. Generic vs none is only about +17%. *MODERATE*: it is a real audit-style design, but run by a vendor, with no significance tests or per-group n published, US only, and no student roles specified ([ResumeGo](https://www.resumego.net/research/cover-letters/)). Several blogs misstate this as "+53% vs generic".
- **Some managers expect a letter even when it's marked optional:** 72% (Resume Genius 2023) and 30.3% (iHire 2020). The spread shows how sensitive these numbers are to question wording. *WEAK-vendor*.
- **Oxford Careers says to submit a letter whenever you're given the chance, but to follow the employer's own length and structure rules first** ([Oxford Careers](https://www.ox.ac.uk/careers/careers-guidance/job-search-and-applications/writing-applications/cover-letters)). *MODERATE* (expert guidance).

### D. Tailoring vs generic

- **Before LLMs, tailoring measurably raised callbacks and pay.** On Freelancer.com, employers paid a premium for more customised applications before LLMs. That premium disappeared after LLMs ([Galdin & Silbert 2025, arXiv:2511.08785](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.08785v1)). *STRONG* (structural econometrics on large platform data).
- **AI-made tailoring loses signal value.** When Freelancer.com's AI Bid Writer rolled out (DiD, about 5.5M bids, 2023), the correlation between how well a letter matched the job post (TF-IDF) and callbacks fell by about 51%, and its correlation with offers by about 79%. Employers moved weight to signals that are hard to fake, such as work history and reviews. Workers who edited the AI draft more had higher callback and offer rates (correlational) ([Cui, Dias & Ye 2025, arXiv:2509.25054](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.25054v2)). *STRONG* for gig platforms, but the authors say generalising to conventional hiring is unproven.
- **Generic letters are recruiters' top complaint.** In ResumeGo's survey, 81% value tailored letters significantly more than generic ones. In Zety's, 72% have rejected applications not customised to the role. MIT CAPD, Oxford, Cambridge and QMUL all name the generic letter and the "CV recap" letter as the main failures. *WEAK-vendor for the numbers, MODERATE for the consensus* ([MIT CAPD](https://capd.mit.edu/resources/how-to-write-an-effective-cover-letter/), [Oxford](https://www.ox.ac.uk/careers/careers-guidance/job-search-and-applications/writing-applications/cover-letters), [Cambridge Careers](https://www.careers.cam.ac.uk/files/cambridgecvsandapplications.pdf)).
- **What recruiters say they look for in a letter** (Zety, single-choice): fit between experience and role 27%, personality and communication 24%, a referral or company connection 19%, evidence of reading the JD 13%, motivation 9%, metrics 4%, keywords 2%. *WEAK-vendor*, but notable: **keywords rank near the bottom, so letters are not an ATS keyword exercise.**

### E. What gets a letter rejected

- **Typos and errors cost interviews.** In a scenario experiment with 445 real recruiters, CVs with many spelling errors had an interview probability 18.5 points lower, and CVs with a few errors 7.3 points lower. About half the penalty came from recruiters inferring lower mental ability, conscientiousness and interpersonal skills ([Sterkens et al., PLOS ONE 2023](https://iza.org/publications/dp/14614/imprint)). *STRONG* for CVs. The mechanism plausibly carries over to letters.
- **Rejection on the letter alone is commonly reported.** 81% of recruiters say they have rejected a candidate based solely on (or on details in) the cover letter. The wording varies by outlet. Zety's general rejection reasons: lies or exaggeration 76%, poor formatting 72%, not customised 72%, typos 68% ([TechRSeries on Zety](https://techrseries.com/recruitment-and-on-boarding/zety-report-finds-81-of-recruiters-have-rejected-a-candidate-based-on-details-in-their-cover-letter/)). *WEAK-vendor*.
- **Overclaiming backfires.** Coding of real résumés and cover letters found that a *moderate* amount of self-promotion and ingratiation raised perceived fit, while *heavy* self-promotion read as disingenuous ([Waung et al., J. Business & Psychology 2017](https://umdearborn.edu/people-um-dearborn/marie-waung); [APS summary](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/news/minds-business/how-much-is-too-much-bragging-on-a-resume.html)). *STRONG* (peer-reviewed lab/coding study).
- **Recruiter practitioners say a bad letter is worse than none, and that weak letters fail to make a clear ask or repeat the CV** ([Hired Guns](https://thehiredguns.com/5-cover-letter-tips-from-a-recruiter/), [4 Corner Resources](https://www.4cornerresources.com/career-advice/cover-letter-mistakes/)). *WEAK* (anecdotal practitioner writing). It is partly at odds with ResumeGo's finding that even generic letters beat none.

### F. AI-written letters (directly relevant to Placed)

- **Polish isn't what wins.** Tilburg University experiments with recruiters from Philips, PwC, VodafoneZiggo and Rabobank found that ChatGPT-assisted letters scored higher on quality but **did not raise interview invitations**. AI improved the standard boilerplate but left the motivation content (which recruiters weight most) largely unchanged ([IO+ on Wicker et al., ESB 2026](https://ioplus.nl/en/posts/ai-improves-cover-letters-not-hiring-chances); registered trial [AEARCTR-0014784](https://www.socialscienceregistry.org/trials/14784)). *STRONG-ish*: the design is preregistered, but this is a press summary and the full results aren't published.
- **Suspected AI use costs the applicant.** In a Tilburg master's thesis, once recruiters suspected ChatGPT use, their rating of that letter dropped, and so did the interview likelihood ([Universonline 2025](https://universonline.nl/nieuws/2025/02/19/chatgpt-in-job-applications-is-using-ai-the-key-to-success-or-a-pitfall/)). *MODERATE* (small student study).
- **Clarity help on its own is fine.** In an RCT with about 480k jobseekers, *non-generative* writing assistance (error fixing and readability) raised hires by 8% and wages by 10%, with no drop in employer satisfaction ([Wiles, Munyikwa & Horton, Management Science 2025](https://www.nber.org/papers/w30886)). *STRONG*. **Polishing the user's own words helps. Generating generic content does not.**
- **AI letters run long.** On Freelancer.com the median human cover letter was 79 words before ChatGPT and 104 after, and the platform's AI tool produced a median of 159 words ([Galdin & Silbert 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.08785v1)). *STRONG* (descriptive).
- **UK context.** Applications per graduate vacancy hit about 140 in 2023/24, a record, partly blamed on AI-assisted mass applying. 43% of ISE employers are fine with AI at some stages ([ISE via Irish News](https://www.irishnews.com/news/uk/competition-for-graduate-jobs-at-record-high-institute-of-student-employers-XQKAURASIBPFTGNWPQMCINGDDY/), [ISE insight](https://ise.org.uk/knowledge/insights/499/how_can_employers_manage_and_adapt_to_mass_applications)). *MODERATE*. UK careers services warn that AI text shares recognisable stock phrasing across applicants ([Exeter Careers](https://exeter.ac.uk/students/careers/research/helpwith/helpwithusingai)). Of UK hiring managers, 80% distrust AI-generated content ([CV Genius via DIGIT](https://www.digit.fyi/80-of-hiring-managers-critical-of-ai-generated-content/)). *WEAK-vendor*.

### G. Length

- **Short wins.** Zety: 49% want half a page, 26% one page, 25% "a few sentences". CV Genius UK: 59–69% prefer concise. Oxford and MIT both cap letters at one side of A4. HBR-linked advice: about one minute to read. *WEAK-vendor for the numbers, MODERATE for the consensus.* Combined with the "under 30 seconds at screen" finding, this supports a target of **about 200–350 words**.

## 2. Implications for an AI cover letter writer

1. **Design for a 10–30 second skim, then a closer read.** The first 2–3 sentences must carry role, company and the strongest specific proof point. Everything else is for the tie-break or interview-stage reader.
2. **Specificity is the whole game, and it has to be hard to fake.** LLM-generated tailoring (keyword overlap with the JD) has already lost about half its signal value. What still carries signal is concrete, verifiable detail from the user's own history (named projects, numbers, people met, events attended) that a generic model couldn't produce. The writer must **ground every claim in the user's CV or vault, and refuse to invent**.
3. **Don't optimise for polish.** Polish doesn't raise invitations, and visible "AI-ness" causes a suspicion penalty. Prefer plain, slightly informal UK graduate register over ornate LLM prose. Block stock phrases.
4. **Motivation is the under-served part.** AI improves boilerplate, but recruiters weight motivation, so the writer should ask the user for (or pull from memory) a genuine reason for this specific firm. A specific, true motivation sentence beats three generic ones.
5. **Moderate self-promotion.** Evidence-backed claims, no superlatives in a row, no "perfect candidate".
6. **Shorter than an LLM wants to write.** Default to about 250 words and hard-cap at one A4 side. LLM output runs about 1.5–2x longer than human letters.
7. **Know when not to write a letter.** Many UK grad schemes (PwC and others) use form questions instead. Placed should detect "no cover letter / form questions" and switch to answering motivation questions with the same evidence. For banks that use a personal-statement format, follow the firm's format exactly.
8. **Zero-error tolerance.** Even a few typos carry a measurable penalty. Wrong company names, leftover placeholders and mismatched role titles are the catastrophic version.
9. **Optional means send.** When a letter is optional, a tailored letter is a modest expected gain and close to no downside. A generic one is close to neutral.

## 3. Candidate rules

| ID | Rule | Why | Severity | Checkable |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CL-EV-01 | The letter must name the exact target company and exact role title, matching the job record, in the first paragraph. | Readers skim for under 30 seconds; naming both signals the letter isn't mass-sent; a wrong company name is an instant reject. | critical | regex (company and role strings in first ~60 words; no other employer name from the user's job list present) |
| CL-EV-02 | No unfilled placeholders or template tokens (`[Company]`, `{{role}}`, "Dear [Name]", "XYZ", "Lorem"). | Like typos, this signals low conscientiousness; recruiters reject on details in the letter. | critical | regex |
| CL-EV-03 | Body length 200–350 words (soft), with a hard cap of 400 words and one A4 side. | Recruiter preference is half a page to one page; LLM letters run 1.5–2x long; skim time is under 30 seconds. | major | length |
| CL-EV-04 | Every factual claim (employer, project, metric, grade, award) must trace to the user's CV, profile or vault; no invented facts. | Lies and exaggeration are the top rejection reason (76%), and interview-stage readers check them. | critical | LLM-judge (claim-vs-source entailment) |
| CL-EV-05 | Include at least 2 concrete, specific proof points (a number, named project, named tool or named outcome) from the user's history. | Hard-to-fake specifics are what still carries signal once AI has flattened generic tailoring. | major | LLM-judge + regex heuristic (digits, proper nouns) |
| CL-EV-06 | Include at least 1 company-specific motivation sentence that couldn't be pasted into a competitor's letter (a named team, deal, product, value, event or person met). | Motivation is weighted most and AI improves it least; "why this firm" is the core of UK form questions. | major | LLM-judge (swap test: does the sentence still work if you replace the company?) |
| CL-EV-07 | Don't restate the CV line by line; each body paragraph must add context, motivation or a link to the role that the CV doesn't show. | The "recap" letter is the second-most-cited failure (MIT, Oxford, HBR). | major | LLM-judge (overlap ratio vs CV text; flag if more than ~60% of sentences paraphrase CV bullets) |
| CL-EV-08 | Ban stock LLM and cliché phrases ("I am writing to express my interest", "perfect fit", "passionate about", "fast-paced environment", "leverage my skills", "delve", "tapestry", "I am confident that"). | Recruiters penalise suspected AI; stock phrasing is the most visible tell and repeats across applicants. | major | regex (maintained blocklist) |
| CL-EV-09 | Moderate self-promotion: no more than 2 unsupported superlatives, and no claim to be "the ideal/perfect candidate". | Heavy self-promotion reads as disingenuous (Waung et al.). | minor | regex + LLM-judge |
| CL-EV-10 | Zero spelling or grammar errors, in UK English spelling throughout. | Even a few errors cut interview probability by about 7 points (Sterkens et al.). | critical | spellcheck / LLM-judge |
| CL-EV-11 | The opening two sentences must contain role + company + the single strongest proof point or motivation hook; no throat-clearing opener. | The first screen is a 10–30 second skim, and generic openers signal low effort. | major | LLM-judge (+ regex ban on "I am writing to…") |
| CL-EV-12 | Address a named person if one is in the job record or the user supplies one; otherwise "Dear Hiring Manager" (never "To whom it may concern" or "Dear Sir/Madam"). | MIT, HBR and UK careers guidance agree; it is a cheap effort signal. | minor | regex |
| CL-EV-13 | Close with a clear, polite forward-looking ask (interview or conversation). | Practitioners report about half of letters never ask. | minor | LLM-judge |
| CL-EV-14 | If the application is form-based (no letter field) or the employer specifies its own format, length or prompts, follow the employer's spec instead of the default letter template. | Many UK grad schemes (e.g. PwC) take no letter; Goldman uses a personal-statement format; Oxford says employer rules come first. | major | not checkable automatically (needs job metadata); partly rule-based on the job's `application_type` |
| CL-EV-15 | When a letter is optional, generate one by default, but always tailor it. Never offer a "generic" mode. | Tailored is about +53% vs none and about +31% vs generic; generic is only about +17% vs none (ResumeGo, vendor). | major | not checkable (product policy) |
| CL-EV-16 | Prefer editing the user's own draft or answers over generating from scratch, and keep the user's voice. | Non-generative clarity help raises hires (Horton RCT); more user editing of AI drafts correlates with callbacks; AI-polished generic text has lost signal. | major | not checkable (UX policy); could log the edit-distance ratio |
| CL-EV-17 | Don't stuff JD keywords; reuse at most a few key JD terms, and only where they describe real experience. | Recruiters rank keywords near the bottom of what they look for; letters aren't the ATS-scored document. | minor | LLM-judge / ratio check (JD n-gram overlap ceiling) |
| CL-EV-18 | If the user has a referral, an event attended or a named employee contact, use it in paragraph 1 or 2. | 19% of recruiters say a connection is what they look for most; it is a hard-to-fake signal. | minor | LLM-judge (if the vault has a contact or event for this company, was it used?) |
| CL-EV-19 | If the CV shows an unexplained gap or a non-obvious pivot, address it briefly (one sentence) in the letter. | Recruiters' preferred place to explain a gap is the letter. | minor | LLM-judge (needs gap detection from the CV) |

## 4. Contradictions and open questions

1. **How much letters matter depends on who asks.** Vendor surveys say 80–90% read or value letters; Jobvite and SHRM say 26–40%. Likely causes: vendor incentives, leading question wording, and respondent mix (hiring managers read more than high-volume recruiters). For high-volume UK grad schemes the low end is probably closer to the truth at first screen. For SMEs, startups and smaller boutiques the letter may matter more.
2. **Is a bad letter worse than none?** Practitioners say yes. ResumeGo's experiment says even a generic letter slightly beats none. Neither tested an actively *bad* letter (typos, wrong company). Working assumption: generic ≥ none > bad.
3. **Does tailoring still pay off in the AI era?** Platform evidence says AI-generated tailoring has lost most of its signal, but that comes from gig markets with very short (~100-word) letters. It is untested in UK graduate schemes, where humans may read the letter at interview stage. Placed should assume only *grounded, specific* tailoring helps.
4. **There is no rigorous data on how often letters are read at interview prep or used as a tie-break.** Every claim about this is anecdotal.
5. **ResumeGo numbers:** per-group callback rates come from secondary reproductions of the charts. The primary page text gives only "+53%", with no significance tests. Recheck before quoting exact percentages in the product UI.
6. **The "81% rejected based on cover letter" figure** is attributed to Zety, ResumeBuilder and others, and worded as either "solely on" or "on details in". Don't use it in user-facing copy.
7. **The AI disclosure trial results (AEARCTR-0014784)** aren't published yet. Watch for them: they would settle whether Placed should advise users to disclose AI help.
8. **UK-specific employer data on cover letters is close to non-existent.** ISE covers volumes and AI misconduct but not letter reading. CV Genius UK is the only UK hiring-manager survey, and it comes from a vendor.
