# 03 — UK Finance & Consulting Cover Letters (and "Why us?" boxes)

Researcher angle: investment banking (IBD), sales & trading / markets, asset management, Big 4 (audit/tax/advisory), strategy consulting (MBB + tier 2), insurance/actuarial. Audience: UK undergrads applying to spring weeks, summer internships, off-cycles, grad schemes.

Researched 2026-10-17. Rule IDs use the prefix `CL-FC-##`.

**How strong is the evidence?** Firm pages are the strongest source but they rarely say much. Bain London, BCG campus pages and TARGETjobs' write-up of the PwC process are first-party or near it. Most "what good looks like" advice comes from specialist sites (TARGETjobs, eFinancialCareers, Mergers & Inquisitions, RocketBlocks, Bright Network member stories). They agree with each other closely, but none of it is measured. Requirements change every cycle and vary by office and division. **Treat every "firm X requires a letter" claim as dated**, and let the posting decide.

**Implication for Placed.** The writer needs **three output modes**, all built on the same "why us / why role / why me" content:
- **(a) Full letter:** one page, 250–450 words.
- **(b) GS-style short letter:** about 300 words.
- **(c) Form/box answer:** respects a word or character limit, has no letter furniture, and answers exactly the question asked.

The posting decides the mode.

## 2. What good looks like, by sub-sector

### Common core (all sectors). Evidence: strong, because sources agree.

- **The three questions: why this firm, why this division/role, why me.** eFC gives a three-paragraph version (firm, division, you) and a five-paragraph version that adds an intro and conclusion ([eFC IB cover letter](https://www.efinancialcareers.co.uk/news/investment-banking-cover-letter)). TARGETjobs reduces it to "Why them?" and "Why you?" plus the choice of division ([TARGETjobs IB](https://targetjobs.co.uk/careers-advice/accountancy-banking-and-finance/how-write-cover-letter-investment-banking-role)).
- **One side of A4 at most, often less.** eFC: "Anything longer than one page is overkill." Ex-GS Afzal Hussein recommends 70–90% of a page ([eFC/Hussein](https://www.efinancialcareers.com/news/2018/05/goldman-sachs-cover-letter)).
- **Not a CV summary.** "A cover letter isn't a CV summary" (eFC). RocketBlocks and Management Consulted say the same.
- **Every claim must be backed on the CV.** "No one cares about unverified skills" (eFC mistakes).
- **Generic praise is the #1 failure.** TARGETjobs' blunder example is "prestigious and vibrant company" ([TARGETjobs blunders](https://targetjobs.co.uk/careers-advice/cvs-applications-and-tests/four-covering-letter-blunders-and-how-avoid-them)). TARGETjobs IB also warns against "dynamic, international, leading business". The Nomura swap test, from a Bright Network member: if you can replace the bank's name with another, you haven't differentiated ([Bright Network Nomura](https://www.brightnetwork.co.uk/member-success-stories/how-i-did-it-bright-network-member-julie-securing-insight-scheme-nomura)).
- **Wrong firm name is a known, cited failure.** eFC cites a letter to JPMorgan that referred to Morgan Stanley. TARGETjobs on McKinsey warns about leaving another firm's name in the text.
- **Don't recite website facts.** Office counts and headcount are out (Hussein/eFC). Don't flatter: "Banks love themselves" is quoted as a warning against over-praise (eFC mistakes).
- **Don't claim attention to detail; show it.** Typos are disqualifying (TARGETjobs blunders; eFC quotes a GS recruiter).

### Investment banking (IBD). Evidence: medium to strong.

- **Structure** ([M&I](https://mergersandinquisitions.com/investment-banking-cover-letter-template/)):
  1. Intro: who you are, university, how you found the bank, and the role. Name-drop contacts and events here.
  2. 1–2 most relevant experiences, with results, in 3–4 sentences.
  3. Fit with the role.
  4. Short close.
- **Calibrate effort.** M&I says letters matter "10x less than resumes" at large US banks, but more at boutiques and in Europe. They act as a **screen**: a bad letter rejects you, a good one rarely carries you. So the AI should optimise for **zero defects plus specificity**, not flourish.
- **Name-dropping is expected.** GS: if you've networked, the letter is the best place to name contacts ([eFC GS](https://www.efinancialcareers.com/news/how-to-get-a-job-goldman-sachs)). A Citi spring-weeker found it valuable to say they'd spoken to a named person at the bank ([Bright Network Citi](https://www.brightnetwork.co.uk/member-success-stories/shiroms-insights-into-securing-an-insights-week-with-citi)). Omitting a referral can look ungrateful (eFC mistakes).
- **Why the division.** Say why M&A, ECM/DCM, Lev Fin, coverage or restructuring, and whether the bank is strong there (TARGETjobs IB). Use league-table position or a recent mandate only if it is verifiable.
- **Why this bank.** Use differentiators: training structure, business mix, regional footprint, a specific sector franchise, insight-day conversations (TARGETjobs IB). TARGETjobs also notes stronger Macquarie applicants had "engaged with recruiters" ([TARGETjobs why us](http://targetjobs.co.uk/careers-advice/accountancy-banking-and-finance/interview-question-why-do-you-want-work-financial-services)).
- **Weak A-levels or reapplying.** GS's EMEA recruitment head said candidates can address these in the cover letter ([eFC 2013](https://www.efinancialcareers.co.uk/news/2013/09/the-emea-head-of-recruitment-at-goldman-sachs-is-here-to-answer-your-questions); [eFC 2011](https://www.efinancialcareers.com/news/2011/07/your-questions-to-the-head-of-emea-recruitment-at-goldman-sachs)). This is old but still the only first-party-adjacent statement found.

### Sales & trading / markets. Evidence: weak to medium (few dedicated sources).

- Show **market engagement**, not prestige: a market view, a product you follow, a trading or investment society role, quantified results (e.g. a portfolio competition rank).
- Barclays' STS programme describes the role as understanding global markets, communication, quantitative and analytical skills ([Bright Network Barclays STS](https://www.brightnetwork.co.uk/graduate-jobs/barclays/sales-trading-and-structuring-graduate-programme-london-2027-allb)). Map evidence to sales vs trading vs structuring.
- A QuantNet reviewer criticised a draft as "too vague" and said the personal origin story belongs in the interview. Replace it with concrete performance ([QuantNet](https://quantnet.com/threads/cover-letter-for-trading.8222/post-77208)).
- Name the **desk family** (rates, FX, credit, equities, commodities, structuring) and why. "Markets" alone reads as undecided.

### Asset management. Evidence: weak.

- Many programmes have no letter. When there is one, the content that differentiates is the **firm's investment approach** (active vs passive, fixed income vs equities strength, stewardship/ESG stance, multi-asset) and the candidate's own **investing evidence** (a stock pitch, investment society, a personal portfolio log, CFA Level I progress).
- Randstad UK: keep it to "a couple of paragraphs", answering why pick me, mapped to the advert's essentials ([Randstad](https://www.randstad.co.uk/career-advice/cover-letter/cv-cover-letter-writing-tips-asset-management-jobs/)).
- Separate **buy-side vs sell-side** motivation explicitly. Generic "finance" motivation reads as an IB reject.

### Big 4 (audit, tax, advisory). Evidence: strong for format, medium for content.

- Usually **no letter**. Motivation is tested in video interview and assessment-centre questions: "Why PwC?", "Why this business area?", "What do you know about PwC?". Answers should link to the firm's values, the work, the clients and the training ([TARGETjobs PwC](http://targetjobs.co.uk/careers-advice/cvs-applications-and-tests/pwc-graduate-application-process-explained)).
- Deloitte's motivation questions check a **realistic understanding of the chosen service line** and alignment with its values ([Intervyo](https://www.intervyo.co.uk/firms/deloitte/hirevue); commercial source, so weak).
- **"Why Big 4" vs "why this Big 4"** is a known interview test: "Why Deloitte as compared to other Big 4" ([Prosple survey](https://au.prosple.com/interviews/big-4-grads-share-the-interview-questions-they-were-asked); Australian data).
- **Values: connect, don't copy.** Name one value and show a time the candidate acted on it. Never list all of a firm's values. (This is inferred from TARGETjobs' guidance to connect firm facts back to yourself, plus the copy-paste concern below. It is not directly sourced.)
- Show understanding of the **professional qualification** (ACA / CTA / ACCA) and of the service line's day-to-day work (audit = testing controls and substantive procedures; tax = compliance plus advisory). This signals realism.

### Strategy consulting (MBB, tier 2). Evidence: medium to strong.

- **Structure:**
  - Intro: name, stage, **office** and role.
  - 1–2 narratives or 2–3 strengths, one paragraph each.
  - Why this firm **and office**.
  - Close.
  - Source: [RocketBlocks](https://www.rocketblocks.me/blog/consulting-cover-letter.php). Management Consulted gives a similar five-paragraph shape: intro, two stories, firm fit, conclusion.
- **Office specificity is mandatory.** The RocketBlocks London sample ties the choice to personal reasons, conversations with consultants, a named practice (digital/TMT) and EMEA cross-office work. Bain: why Bain, why that office, what perspective you bring ([resumegeni](https://resumegeni.com/companies/bain-uk/how-to-apply), weak).
- **Use the firm's own language carefully.** RocketBlocks contrasts McKinsey's emphasis on personal impact with Bain's "one team". Don't spend paragraphs on size or reputation.
- **McKinsey UK.** Use the letter for distinctive leadership (e.g. society president, with scale), reapplication or unusual circumstances. Show understanding of the BA job (client interviews, analysis, modelling, presentations). Focus on "the firm's values and work", not perks ([TARGETjobs McKinsey](http://targetjobs.co.uk/careers-advice/business-consulting-and-management/applying-mckinsey-company-cvs-cover-letters-and-interviews)).
- **Fatal "why consulting" answer.** IGotAnOffer calls it a "HUGE mistake" to say you want a few years in consulting before deciding what to do. Firms hire future partners ([IGotAnOffer](https://igotanoffer.com/blogs/mckinsey-case-interview-blog/consulting-cover-letter)).

### Insurance / actuarial. Evidence: weak to medium.

- Mirror the person specification point by point, with evidence and ideally numbers ([theactuaryjobs](https://www.theactuaryjobs.com/article/writing-a-compelling-cover-letter-for-actuary-roles-/)).
- Cover why the role, why this company (with research shown), and why you. Generic letters are easy to spot ([actuarialcareers](https://actuarialcareers.co.uk/finding-the-right-job/tips-for-applying-to-actuarial-jobs)).
- Show commitment to **IFoA exams**: exemptions held or exams sat. Name the line of business (life, pensions, GI, reinsurance, Lloyd's market) and why.
- TARGETjobs tests commercial awareness on these forms via "why us" and "what makes you suited", e.g. RBS's "what excites you about the programme" ([TARGETjobs commercial awareness](https://targetjobs.co.uk/careers-advice/accountancy-banking-and-finance/commercial-awareness-what-retail-banking)).

### How to talk about spring weeks, societies and part-time jobs. Evidence: medium.

- **Spring week or insight day at the same firm:** the strongest "why us" asset. Name the division you rotated through, one person or session, and what changed your mind. A BAML spring-weeker used the week to compare S&T with Corporate Treasury ([Bright Network BAML](https://www.brightnetwork.co.uk/member-success-stories/emilys-experience-spring-week-intern-baml)).
- **Spring week at a competitor:** usable as evidence of skills and interest in the sector. Never imply the target firm is a fallback.
- **Societies:** use **scale and outcome** (members, budget, sponsorship £, events run), not the title alone. Cited in TARGETjobs McKinsey (society president example) and TARGETjobs IB ("extracurricular pursuits"). GS's Vicki Tung warns that many minor activities can look like résumé-building (eFC GS). Pick 1–2.
- **Part-time or retail jobs:** frame them as client service, reliability under pressure, or cash handling. TARGETjobs IB suggests a brief customer-service example where a bank stresses client relationships. Balancing work with study is evidence of time management.

### Commercial-awareness signals

TARGETjobs says recruiters test commercial awareness through the form, CV and letter: the firm's priorities, its competitors, its clients, and global events that affect it ([TARGETjobs IB commercial awareness](https://targetjobs.co.uk/careers-advice/accountancy-banking-and-finance/impress-banking-and-investment-graduate)).

Good signals in a letter:
- One recent firm development tied to the role: a deal, product, strategy or results theme.
- One sector trend: rates, private credit growth, AI in audit, ESG regulation.
- Knowing who the competitors are.

Be ready for follow-up on anything mentioned (TARGETjobs why-us).

## 3. "Why this firm" research recipe, for the AI

Input: job description (JD), company name, division, office, the candidate's profile (CV, events attended, contacts, spring weeks).

1. **Classify the posting.**
   - Sector: IBD / S&T / AM / Big 4 / consulting / insurance.
   - Programme: spring week / summer / off-cycle / grad.
   - Office.
   - Output mode: full letter, short letter (~300 words) or box answer with a limit.
   - If the JD says "no cover letter", don't produce one. Offer "why us" interview notes instead.
2. **Extract JD anchors.** Pull the division or service-line name, 3–5 required skills, and any programme features (rotations, ACA/CFA support, international placements, mentoring). These are the only firm facts that are guaranteed true.
3. **Pull candidate-owned touchpoints first. These beat any web fact.**
   - Events and spring weeks at this firm.
   - People spoken to (name and role only if the user supplied them).
   - Society sponsorships by this firm.
   - Alumni from the candidate's university.
   - If there are none, say so honestly and lean on division fit, not flattery.
4. **Find 1–2 differentiators the firm has and its closest competitor lacks.** Use at most one of each kind:
   - (a) A recent deal, mandate or project in the target division, from the firm's newsroom or press. Must include date and source.
   - (b) A sector franchise or region strength.
   - (c) Programme structure.
   - (d) A strategy line from annual results.
   - Run the **competitor swap test**: replace the firm name with its closest rival. If the sentence still holds, discard it.
5. **Map each differentiator to the candidate.** Pattern: "[firm-specific fact] → why it matters for the work I'd do in [division] → evidence I've done related work." No orphan facts. TARGETjobs McKinsey: connect firm facts back to your own experience.
6. **Values (Big 4 / consulting only).** Pick **one** stated value and illustrate it with one behaviour from the CV. Never paraphrase the values page.
7. **Verify.** Every deal or number must have a source URL and a date. Flag anything over 12 months old. If no verifiable fact exists, omit it rather than invent one. Hallucinated deals are worse than none.
8. **Commercial hook (optional).** One sentence on a sector trend that affects this division, which the candidate can defend in interview.

## 4. Candidate rules

**Severity scale**
- **block:** must not ship.
- **high:** fix before ship.
- **med:** warn.
- **low:** style.

**"Check" column**
- **Y:** machine-checkable.
- **P:** partially machine-checkable (heuristic or LLM judge).
- **N:** human or LLM judgment only.

## 5. Open questions

1. **Current-cycle requirements:** which banks require, make optional, or have dropped the letter for 2026/27 (GS, JPM, MS, Citi, BofA, Barclays, UBS, DB, Nomura, HSBC, Lazard, Rothschild, Evercore)? Evidence is anecdotal and conflicting. Placed should **parse the live posting**, not hardcode.
2. **Do letters get read?** M&I says they are a screen only. Some recruiters "love them" (eFC GS). There is no UK quantitative data. Is a letter's marginal value at bulge brackets close to zero once it's defect-free?
3. **AI-use policies:** Bain London restricts AI to CV spelling and grammar and bans it in assessments. Do other firms' T&Cs restrict AI-drafted letters or answers? Placed may need a per-firm "AI policy" warning and a "draft, then you rewrite" UX.
4. **Box answers vs letters:** typical UK word and character limits per firm aren't documented publicly. Could Placed crowd-source them from users' applications?
5. **Values and differentiator database:** should Placed maintain a curated per-firm fact sheet (divisions, programme features, recent deals with dates, rival list) to power CL-FC-07/09/11/12? Who maintains freshness?
6. **Tier-2 consulting and AM:** almost no specialist evidence was found (Oliver Wyman, LEK, Kearney, OC&C; BlackRock, Schroders, M&G). Needs a firm-page sweep.
7. **Named contacts and privacy:** naming a person met at an event is recommended. Should the AI ask consent or confirmation before including a third party's name?
8. **The Gradroom, WSO and Rare-specific guidance** wasn't retrievable in this pass. Firecrawl credits ran out, and LSE and some specialist pages were blocked or 403'd.
