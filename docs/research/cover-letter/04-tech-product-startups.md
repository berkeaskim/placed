# 04 — Tech, Product, Data, Engineering & Startup Applications

Researcher angle: big tech, scale-ups, startups, quant dev/research (brief), plus UK public sector/policy as a contrast case.
Audience: the Placed AI cover letter writer (UK students: internships, spring weeks, grad schemes).
Date: 2026-10-17. Tooling note: Firecrawl ran out of credits, so this used WebSearch/WebFetch only. Some claims rest on search snippets rather than full-page reads; these are marked *(snippet)*.

**Evidence grades:** **A** = the employer's or platform's own published text. **B** = a named practitioner (founder, hiring manager, investor) writing first-hand. **C** = a reputable careers body (TARGETjobs, gradireland, Bright Network, Gradcracker). **D** = vendor or SEO blog, or an anonymous forum post. Rules below lean on A/B where they can.

## 1. Landscape: company type → is a letter expected?

| Company type (examples) | Letter expected? | What the ATS actually shows | Recommended format & length | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Big tech, early careers** (Google, Amazon, Meta, Microsoft) | **Usually not.** Google's student guidance lists CV + transcript, with a cover letter or short essay only "in some cases" when the posting asks *(snippet)*. | **UK grad schemes in IT/engineering** (Gradcracker / TARGETjobs employers) | **Sometimes.** TARGETjobs says "several IT graduate employers" ask for one *(snippet)*. | Employer portals (often Oleeo/Workday) | Max 1 page. **Takeaway for the generator:** in tech, the first decision is **which artifact to produce**: none, a short note, an answer to a specific field, or a full letter. That choice should depend on whether the form has a field and what the field says. Defaulting to a 400-word corporate letter is wrong for most tech applications.

## 2. What good looks like per track

### 2.1 Software engineering (SWE intern / new grad)
- **Proof over adjectives.** HN hiring managers say they are sick of "hard worker" and "great problem solver" claims ([HN 2010](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=1783915), B). Fried says he only digs into a candidate's work if the letter impresses him, and he wants the work to be visible ([37signals](https://37signals.com/podcast/the-case-for-cover-letters/), B).
- **Frame work as impact, not stack.** Patrick McKenzie: engineers are "hired to create business value, not to program things". Describe accomplishments in revenue/cost/user terms, because managers care little about specific tools ([kalzumeus](https://www.kalzumeus.com/2011/10/28/dont-call-yourself-a-programmer/), B). For students, translate this to users, scale, latency, adoption, or a grade or ranking.
- **Show you read the job description.** Doing that and making no errors puts you in the "top 5%" (HN, B). Amazon recruiters say the basic qualifications "are there for a reason" ([Amazon](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/workplace/applying-at-amazon-7-tips-from-recruiters), A).
- **Link the artifact.** Give one GitHub repo, live demo, or write-up per claim, and only links the student actually owns. Startup guides consistently favour a "proof of work" link over a CV restatement ([fueler](https://fueler.io/blog/how-to-land-your-first-startup-internship), D; [Buckhouse](https://jamesbuckhouse.substack.com/p/how-to-find-a-job-at-a-startup), B).
- **Don't list languages.** That belongs in the CV. Keyword-matching is a weak reason to repeat them ([Springboard](https://www.springboard.com/blog/software-engineering/software-engineer-cover-letter/), D, says it helps ATS. Treat this as low priority for big tech where there is no letter field anyway).
- **Strength:** B (consistent across practitioners), little A.

### 2.2 Data / analytics / data science
- **Lead with a question and end on a decision.** "Hiring managers assume you can run a query"; what they want is "what happened because you did" ([Jobscan](https://www.jobscan.co/cover-letter-examples/data-analyst), D, but consistent with McKenzie's business-value framing, B).
- **Use one or two stories that go from raw data to a decision**, each with problem → method → outcome ([Edgewood](https://online.edgewood.edu/blog/how-to-build-a-data-analyst-portfolio/), D).
- **Link a notebook, dashboard, or repo** with a stated business question, not a Titanic or Iris tutorial. *(Inference; no primary source found that names Kaggle tutorials as a negative. See open questions.)*
- **Strength:** C/D. Weakest-evidenced track; rules should stay soft.

### 2.3 Product management (APM / PM intern)
- **Include a user insight about *their* product.** Guides converge on closing with "a specific perspective on the company's product", such as a feature you used plus a hypothesis ([ResumeGeni](https://resumegeni.com/cover-letter-guides/product-manager), D), and on opening with a recent launch ([Product School](https://productschool.com/blog/job-search-advice/how-to-write-a-product-manager-cover-letter-that-will-get-you-hired), D).
- **Avoid PM adjectives.** Product School: every PM claims to be "results-driven" or "a team player" (D).
- **Length:** 3–4 short paragraphs, one page (Product School, D). For startups, use the short note.
- **Show you used it.** Make a concrete, verifiable usage claim (what you did in the product, what broke or delighted, what you'd test). It must come from user-supplied facts. The generator must never invent usage.
- **Strength:** D for PM-specific advice. B-grade support only by analogy: Buckhouse's "A+ no notes" example built something that applied the recipient's own ideas.

### 2.4 Startup generalist / founding-team / ops / growth
- **Format is a short note, not a letter.** Buckhouse (Sequoia design partner): "Send less than 50-100 words", most of them about the recipient. Skip the backstory and credentials, and make an easy ask ([Substack](https://jamesbuckhouse.substack.com/p/how-to-find-a-job-at-a-startup), B). Wellfound: qualify yourself in the first two lines, no jargon, quantify, don't restate the CV ([Wellfound](https://help.wellfound.com/article/793-how-do-i-write-an-effective-note-to-send-with-my-application), A). HN: "Three paragraphs maximum"; "One minute good. Thirty seconds better." ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=1783915), B).
- **Explain the "why" behind the CV.** Cover changes of industry, size, or role, and why this company ([Built In](https://builtin.com/articles/cover-letter-gets-start-ups-call-you), D).
- **Make it about the company, not you.** Starting sentences with "I like" or "I want" is a warning sign (HN commenter, B).
- **Do one small piece of work for them** (onboarding audit, landing-page teardown, prototype) when the student has done it. This was the strongest signal in Buckhouse's examples.
- **Strength:** A+B. The best-evidenced track.

### 2.5 Other: quant, AI labs, policy
- **Quant (Jane Street-style):** no letter. The free text covers why this firm, how you heard, and timeline ([Jane Street](https://www.janestreet.com/join-jane-street/apply/8632392002/), A). Keep it factual and short. Puzzle, competition, or olympiad results go in the CV, not in prose.
- **AI labs:** the "Why X?" field is a real screen. Name specific work and tie it to what you'd contribute. Anthropic's guidance: write the first draft yourself, use AI "to refine your ideas, not replace them", never invent experience ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/candidate-ai-guidance), A).
- **UK Civil Service / policy:** use STAR per behaviour, explain context, and respect the word limit ([gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/success-profiles/success-profiles-candidate-overview), A). This is a separate artifact type from a letter.

## 3. Templates

### 3.1 Startup short note (Wellfound Note / WaaS message / founder email) — target 60–150 words

```
Hi {FounderFirstName | "{Company} team"},

{Line 1: what I do + strongest relevant proof, ≤25 words. e.g. "I'm a 2nd-year UCL CS student; I built {project} — {metric/outcome}."}
{Line 2: the specific reason it's {Company}: a product detail, launch, blog post or problem I have firsthand experience of. ≤30 words.}
{Line 3 (optional): one more proof point mapped to the role's main need, with a link.}

{Proof links: 1–2 URLs the user owns — repo, demo, write-up.}

{Low-friction ask: "Happy to do a short take-home or talk for 15 minutes — is the {Role} role still open for {start date}?"}

{FirstName}
```

Constraints: no "Dear Sir/Madam", no "I am writing to apply", no adjectives without evidence. Plain text, no headers or bullets. The body goes in the message, not an attachment.

### 3.2 Field answer ("Why us?" / "Additional information" / quant free-text) — 50–300 words, bounded by the form

```
{Specific hook about the company: named product/research/engineering post + why it matters to me (1–2 sentences)}
{Bridge to my evidence: 1–2 concrete things I've done that relate to that hook, with outcome}
{What I'd want to work on / contribute in this role (1 sentence)}
{Logistics if asked: how I heard, timeline/visa/start date — plain facts}
```

### 3.3 Full letter for corporates / grad schemes (when a letter is requested) — 250–400 words, ≤1 page

```
Para 1 (2–3 sentences): Role + programme name + year/degree + one-line thesis of fit
  (the thesis = top 2 requirements from the posting I can evidence).
Para 2 (evidence 1): requirement A → my example (situation, what I did, measurable result, link).
Para 3 (evidence 2): requirement B → second example. For Amazon: name the Leadership Principle it shows
  (e.g. Ownership, Dive Deep) in the posting's own wording, max 2 LPs.
Para 4 (why this employer): one specific, verifiable reason (product, tech blog post, team, programme
  structure) — not "industry leader" / "innovative".
Close (1–2 sentences): availability + polite ask. Sign-off.
```

## 4. Candidate rules

Severity: **block** = do not output if violated; **warn** = flag or regenerate; **style** = preference.
"Checkable" = the rule can be enforced with a deterministic or lightweight-LLM check.

| ID | Rule | Why | Severity | Machine-checkable? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CL-TP-01 | **Choose the artifact type before writing:** `none` / `short_note` / `field_answer` / `full_letter`, based on the target form (field present? label? char limit?) and company type. Default to `short_note` for startups, `field_answer` when a "why us"/additional-info field exists, `none` for big-tech early careers without a field. | Most tech forms have no letter field or only a short box (Google, Jane Street, Monzo). A long letter there is wasted or harmful. | block | Yes: rule table on company type + form field metadata |
| CL-TP-02 | **Respect the field's length limit, and stay inside type bands otherwise:** short_note 50–150 words; field_answer ≤ field limit (default 300); full_letter 250–400 words, ≤1 page. | Buckhouse <100 words; HN "three paragraphs max"; gradireland one page. | block (over limit) / warn (band) | Yes: word/char count |
| CL-TP-03 | **First two lines must state who the candidate is + their strongest relevant proof.** No throat-clearing openers ("I am writing to…", "My name is…", "I hope this finds you well"). | Wellfound: qualify yourself "in the first two lines". Fried/HN dislike generic openers. | block | Yes: regex blocklist on the opener + LLM check that line 1 contains a concrete claim |
| CL-TP-04 | **Ban unsupported trait adjectives** ("passionate", "hard-working", "team player", "results-driven", "great problem solver", "detail-oriented") unless the same sentence carries an evidence clause (number, named project, link). | HN managers are tired of these; Product School flags "results-driven"/"team player". | warn | Yes: lexicon + same-sentence evidence check |
| CL-TP-05 | **Every claim must trace to user data** (CV, profile, user notes). Never invent projects, metrics, product usage, or links. | Anthropic forbids creating experiences; fabricated specifics are exposed at interview; trust. | block | Partly: entity/number/URL grounding against source docs |
| CL-TP-06 | **Include ≥1 concrete company-specific hook** (named product, feature, launch, eng/research blog post, programme detail) that is *not* a generic attribute ("innovative", "industry leader", "fast-growing"). | Fried: you can tell when it's being blasted out; HN: cite recent company news; gradireland: one "why this employer" paragraph. | block | Partly: require a named entity from the company research input; blocklist generic praise |
| CL-TP-07 | **Hook must be verifiable from the provided company context.** If the research is empty, ask the user for a reason rather than fabricating one. | Prevents hallucinated launches or features. | block | Yes: hook string must match the research corpus |
| CL-TP-08 | **Tech/product/data roles: include 1–2 proof links when the user has them** (GitHub, demo, portfolio, write-up), placed next to the claim they support, max 3 links total. | Proof-of-work is the strongest startup signal; Fried wants to see the work. | warn (if links exist but are unused) | Yes: URL presence + count + origin = user profile |
| CL-TP-09 | **Express impact as outcome, not stack:** each evidence sentence should name a result (users, latency, accuracy, £/time saved, rank, grade) rather than only listing technologies. | McKenzie: business value over "programmer"; Amazon: quantify; Jobscan: "what happened because you did". | warn | Partly: per-evidence-sentence check for a number/outcome term |
| CL-TP-10 | **Don't restate the CV.** At most one tech-stack list. No chronological job history. Prose should add the *why* or the *story* the CV can't show. | Wellfound: "don't repeat your job history"; Built In: explain the "why"; HN: use the letter for what the CV can't say. | warn | Partly: n-gram overlap with CV text > threshold |
| CL-TP-11 | **Startup notes: end with one specific, low-friction ask** (15-minute call, take-home, "is the role still open for {date}?"). Never "pick your brain"; no multiple asks. | Buckhouse: easy-to-accept asks get replies, vague or large asks fail. | warn | Yes: closing-sentence classifier / single question mark in the close |
| CL-TP-12 | **Startup notes: plain text, informal greeting** ("Hi {Name}" / "Hi {Company} team"), no letterhead, address block, or date. In the email body, not an attachment. | Built In "Hi Gem Team" rewrite; HN: letter in the email body. | style | Yes: format check |
| CL-TP-13 | **Corporates/grad schemes: greet a named person if known, else "Dear {Company} Recruitment Team"**; never "To whom it may concern" or "Dear Sir/Madam". | HN: a letter "that doesn't even have my name in it"; Fried tolerates "Dear hiring manager" only if the rest is specific. | warn | Yes: regex |
| CL-TP-14 | **Company-over-self ratio:** at most ~30% of sentences start with "I", and none start "I want"/"I like" in the opening paragraph. | HN: "about the company, not about you"; Buckhouse: most words should be about the recipient. | style | Yes: sentence-start counter |
| CL-TP-15 | **Amazon (and other values-led firms): map evidence to at most 2 named principles from the employer's published list,** using the exact principle name and backing each with a concrete example. | LP mapping is consistently advised (D sources); naming 2 concretely beats listing all 16. | warn | Yes: company → values list; count + example adjacency |
| CL-TP-16 | **PM roles: include one user-level product observation** (what the user did in the product, plus one hypothesis or improvement), sourced only from user-supplied usage notes. If there are no notes, prompt the user to add one. | PM guides converge on product-specific insight; it shows product sense; must be real. | warn | Partly: requires a `product_usage` input; presence check |
| CL-TP-17 | **Data roles: each evidence story = question → method → decision/outcome**; tool names are subordinate. | Jobscan/Edgewood structure; McKenzie impact framing. | style | LLM-checkable |
| CL-TP-18 | **Quant / "additional info" boxes: factual, ≤150 words, cover only why-this-firm, how-heard, and timeline/constraints.** No narrative intro. | Jane Street's form lists exactly those prompts and says it doesn't need a letter. | warn | Yes: length + topic slots |
| CL-TP-19 | **Civil Service/policy statements are a separate artifact:** one STAR example per named behaviour, context explained, advert word limit enforced. | gov.uk Success Profiles guidance. | block (wrong artifact) | Yes: route by employer type; per-behaviour section check |
| CL-TP-20 | **Anti-template signal:** no two letters for the same user (across companies) may share >40% of sentences verbatim; re-generate the hook and evidence ordering. | Fried: phrasing patterns across many applications reveal machine or blasted text; HN template ≈40% max. | warn | Yes: cross-letter similarity |
| CL-TP-21 | **Zero spelling/grammar errors; UK spelling for UK employers.** | Wellfound, gradireland, HN: errors read as carelessness, and some managers rule out on this alone. | block | Yes: spell/grammar lint with en-GB locale |
| CL-TP-22 | **User-authored seed:** require at least one sentence or reason from the user (e.g. "why this company, in your words") and keep its substance. Frame the output as a refinement, not a ghost-write. | Anthropic: draft first, AI refines; Fried: "change 5% of it, it's not yours". Protects authenticity. | warn | Yes: presence of a user seed + semantic overlap with the output |
| CL-TP-23 | **Don't generate a letter just because an optional upload exists**, unless the user has a specific hook (CL-TP-06) *and* one non-CV proof point. Otherwise recommend skipping. | HireFlow: optional template uploads become a negative signal on high-volume reqs; Built In: only write if you have something specific to say. | warn | Yes: preconditions check |

## 5. Open questions

1. **Do big-tech UK early-careers portals (Google, Meta, Microsoft, Bloomberg) currently have any free-text field?** Unconfirmed for Bloomberg London SWE intern and Meta. Placed should read the field list from the actual form (Autopilot already sees forms) rather than hardcode this.
2. **The "Why Anthropic" word count (200–400)** comes from third-party prep sites. The live form should be checked; the same applies to other AI labs' "Why X" fields.
3. **Do startup founders prefer personalised notes or volume?** One bootcamp-grad blogger argues for A/B-tested mass emails over personalisation ([Stern](https://stern.bearblog.dev/yc-startup-coding-bootcamp/), D); A/B sources say tailor. Our rules choose tailoring, but this could be tested on reply rates in Placed's own data.
4. **No primary YC Work at a Startup guidance** on the candidate message was found. It's worth checking the WaaS candidate FAQ when logged in.
5. **Data/PM-specific guidance is weak (D-grade).** We need hiring-manager writing (e.g. Lenny's Newsletter, Reforge, Monzo/Deliveroo data blogs) to firm up CL-TP-16/17.
6. **Does the ATS keyword case matter for letters?** Greenhouse/Lever show the letter next to the CV for human review; there's no evidence that letters are keyword-scored in tech ATS. For now this is treated as not mattering.
7. **AI-detection risk:** recruiters say they flag "generic" rather than "AI" ([Coursera](https://www.coursera.org/articles/ai-cover-letter-red-flags-recruiters-spot-fast-video), D). Self-reported survey numbers conflict (53%–88%). CL-TP-20/22 are our mitigation; whether they work is unmeasured.
8. **Gradcracker's own cover letter article** couldn't be read (403). The TARGETjobs IT article now redirects to a general IT page. Both are worth a manual read for UK engineering-specific norms.
