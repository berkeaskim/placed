# 02 — Structure and Writing Craft of an Excellent Cover Letter

Researcher angle: structure, paragraph jobs, openings, evidence, length, tone, salutation, formatting.
Audience: UK university students applying to internships, spring weeks, and graduate schemes.
Compiled: 2026-10-17. Sources: 19 (list in section 6). Quotes are kept to short phrases; everything else is paraphrased.

> Evidence strength key
> - **STRONG**: 4+ independent reputable sources agree (university careers services, recognised experts), no credible dissent.
> - **MODERATE**: 2–3 reputable sources agree, or broad agreement with notable dissent or variation.
> - **WEAK**: a single source, convention without evidence, or a contested or unverified empirical claim.

---

## 1. Recommended skeleton

Target total: **250–400 words in the body** (hard ceiling: one side of A4 at 10–12pt, which is about 450 words). The four-part structure (opening, why you, why them, close) is the near-universal UK model ([LSE](http://info.lse.ac.uk/current-students/careers/information-and-resources/application-process/cover-letters), [TARGETjobs](http://targetjobs.co.uk/careers-advice/cvs-applications-and-tests/covering-letter-essentials-graduate-vacancies), [Glasgow](https://www.gla.ac.uk/myglasgow/careers/career-development/graduate-information/applying-for-graduate-jobs/graduate-cover-letters), [Prospects](https://www.prospects.ac.uk/careers-advice/cvs-and-cover-letters/cover-letters), [Essex](https://www.essex.ac.uk/student/careers/cover-letters)).

| # | Section | Job of the section | Target words | Must contain |
|---|---------|--------------------|--------------|--------------|
| 0 | Header | Identify sender, date, recipient, role ref | (not counted) | Name, email, phone, date, company, role title, reference number if the advert has one |
| 1 | Salutation | Address a human | (not counted) | Named person if known, otherwise "Dear [Team] team" or "Dear Hiring Manager" (see 2.9) |
| 2 | Opening / hook | Say what you are applying for and give one specific reason to keep reading | 40–70 | Exact role title (plus programme/division/office); one concrete hook tying *you* to *this* role or firm; optionally how you heard of it or a named contact |
| 3 | Why me (evidence) | Prove fit against 2–3 top requirements | 120–200 (1–2 paragraphs) | 2–3 requirements from the advert, each backed by a specific example with action and result (number or outcome where possible), each linked forward to the role |
| 4 | Why them | Show informed, specific motivation | 60–100 | 1–2 facts specific to this employer (business area, deal, product, training structure, values) **connected to your own experience or goals**; must fail the "swap the company name" test |
| 5 | Close / call to action | Summarise the fit, show forward momentum | 25–50 | One-sentence summary of what you would bring, availability or interest in interview, thanks; no generic filler |
| 6 | Sign-off | Conventional UK close | (not counted) | "Yours sincerely" (named recipient) or "Yours faithfully" ("Dear Sir/Madam"); see 2.9 for "Dear Hiring Manager" |

Notes on ordering:
- Sections 3 and 4 may be swapped ([TARGETjobs](http://targetjobs.co.uk/careers-advice/cvs-applications-and-tests/covering-letter-essentials-graduate-vacancies)). LSE puts "why them" before "why me" ([LSE](http://info.lse.ac.uk/current-students/careers/information-and-resources/application-process/cover-letters)). Prospects puts skills first ([Prospects](https://www.prospects.ac.uk/careers-advice/cvs-and-cover-letters/cover-letters)). For a writer, **put the stronger section first**. A student with strong evidence leads with "why me". A student with thin experience but real knowledge of the firm leads with "why them".
- The Careers Group (University of London) advises *weaving* motivation and evidence together rather than in rigidly separate blocks (via [Essex/RVC search summary](https://www.rvc.ac.uk/Media/Default/Alumni/CCH%2021%20Cover%20letters.pdf)). This is a reasonable "advanced" mode, but the separate-block structure is safer for an AI default.
- Paragraph count: 4–5 body paragraphs total (3–5 per [Prospects](https://www.prospects.ac.uk/careers-advice/cvs-and-cover-letters/cover-letters)). Each paragraph should be short, because readers skim ([Glasgow](https://www.gla.ac.uk/myglasgow/careers/career-development/graduate-information/applying-for-graduate-jobs/graduate-cover-letters)). Aim for ≤ 5 sentences or about 100 words per paragraph.

Word-count variants:
- **Online form "cover letter" box with a character limit**: obey the limit. Compress to hook (1–2 sentences), 2 evidence sentences per requirement, 1 why-them sentence, 1 close.
- **Spring week / first-year insight**: lower bound (~250 words). Evidence comes from academics, societies, part-time work, and competitions. Why-them carries more weight.
- **Speculative application**: no advert exists, so infer 2–3 requirements from research ([LSE](http://info.lse.ac.uk/current-students/careers/information-and-resources/application-process/cover-letters)). The close asks for a conversation or for consideration for future openings.

## 2. Key principles

### 2.1 Tailor to one employer and one role. Generic letters fail. — STRONG
- Every source agrees. LSE gives an operational test: if the letter still works with another employer's name swapped in, it is not specific enough ([LSE](http://info.lse.ac.uk/current-students/careers/information-and-resources/application-process/cover-letters)). TARGETjobs lists a generic letter sent to everyone as a top mistake ([TARGETjobs](http://targetjobs.co.uk/careers-advice/cvs-applications-and-tests/covering-letter-essentials-graduate-vacancies)). Prospects warns against copied templates ([Prospects](https://www.prospects.ac.uk/careers-advice/cvs-and-cover-letters/cover-letters)). MIT tells students to customise each letter using the job description, company site, and LinkedIn ([MIT CAPD](https://capd.mit.edu/resources/how-to-write-an-effective-cover-letter/)). LSE also says employers recognise templates.
- Agreement: total. No source defends a generic letter.

### 2.2 Do not restate the CV. Add context, depth, and traits the CV cannot show. — STRONG
- Alison Green calls summarising the résumé the most common mistake. The CV is already attached, so the letter should add what it can't, such as work habits and how you operate ([Ask a Manager 2017](https://www.askamanager.org/2017/06/how-to-write-a-cover-letter-that-will-help-you-get-an-interview.html), [2018](https://www.askamanager.org/2018/05/how-to-write-a-great-cover-letter.html)). LSE warns against a "prose version" of the CV ([LSE](http://info.lse.ac.uk/current-students/careers/information-and-resources/application-process/cover-letters)). TARGETjobs says to rephrase rather than copy CV wording ([TARGETjobs](http://targetjobs.co.uk/careers-advice/cvs-applications-and-tests/covering-letter-essentials-graduate-vacancies)). MIT and GMU agree: go deeper on one experience, or group experiences under a skill ([MIT CAPD](https://capd.mit.edu/resources/how-to-write-an-effective-cover-letter/), [GMU Writing Center](https://writingcenter.gmu.edu/guides/writing-cover-letters)).
- Implication for AI: the generator has the CV in context and will be tempted to paraphrase bullets. It must instead pick 2–3 items and expand the *how* and *why*.

### 2.3 Evidence per claim ("show, don't tell"). — STRONG
- GMU says not to simply claim skills but to show them, including traits like enthusiasm ([GMU](https://writingcenter.gmu.edu/guides/writing-cover-letters)). LSE says to back up every claim with an example of when and how you used the skill ([LSE](http://info.lse.ac.uk/current-students/careers/information-and-resources/application-process/cover-letters)). Prospects agrees ([Prospects](https://www.prospects.ac.uk/careers-advice/cvs-and-cover-letters/cover-letters)). UCSB recommends STAR plus a "So what?" step ([UCSB](https://career.ucsb.edu/get-hired/cover-letters/polish-the-writing)). Principal Center calls lists of unsupported claims the worst offenders ([Principal Center](https://dashboard.principalcenter.com/?p=4659)).
- Western Sydney adds a voice rule: write "I did" rather than "I would" ([WSU](https://www.westernsydney.edu.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0020/2006615/Application_Tips.pdf)).
- Agreement: total. This is the single most rule-shaped principle in the literature.

### 2.4 Map 2–4 requirements from the advert to proof. — STRONG (on count: MODERATE)
- LSE: pick 3–4 key skills from the person specification and give detailed examples ([LSE](http://info.lse.ac.uk/current-students/careers/information-and-resources/application-process/cover-letters)). GMU suggests building a requirements-to-evidence table before drafting ([GMU](https://writingcenter.gmu.edu/guides/writing-cover-letters)). Stanford-adjacent guides suggest 2–3 experiences (via [search summary of US careers guides](https://sc.edu/about/offices_and_divisions/career_center/documents/students_cover_letter_guidelines.pdf)). Careerminds says one or two well-developed examples ([Careerminds](https://blog.careerminds.com/blog/showing-not-just-telling-that-youre-the-best)). HSPH says to use keywords from the posting ([Harvard HSPH](https://www.hsph.harvard.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/36/2016/06/Cover-letter-writing-guide-Feb-25-2016.pdf)).
- Disagreement: the count ranges from 1–2 (Careerminds) to 3–4 (LSE). At 250–400 words, **2–3 developed examples** is the practical sweet spot. Four examples force shallow ones.

### 2.5 "Why them" must be specific and connected to you, not copied from the website. — STRONG
- LSE says to link what you learned to your own experience rather than repeat the employer's website ([LSE](http://info.lse.ac.uk/current-students/careers/information-and-resources/application-process/cover-letters)). TARGETjobs warns against vague praise that could apply to any employer ([TARGETjobs](http://targetjobs.co.uk/careers-advice/cvs-applications-and-tests/covering-letter-essentials-graduate-vacancies)). Glasgow says to link your values or goals to theirs ([Glasgow](https://www.gla.ac.uk/myglasgow/careers/career-development/graduate-information/applying-for-graduate-jobs/graduate-cover-letters)). Oxford, via the Keble JCR summary, says to find what makes the culture distinctive ([Oxford via Keble JCR](https://jcr.keble.ox.ac.uk/?p=2020)). The TARGETjobs Deutsche Bank guide says to research culture and business areas ([TARGETjobs DB](https://targetjobs.co.uk/employers/deutsche-bank/deutsche-bank-internship-cover-letter-tips-469940)).
- Practical form: "[specific fact about firm] + [why it matters to me, tied to a past experience or concrete goal]".

### 2.6 Openings: state the role, then hook. Do not waste the first line. — MODERATE
- Convergent view: the first paragraph names the role (and the advert reference) and gives a specific reason the reader should care ([LSE](http://info.lse.ac.uk/current-students/careers/information-and-resources/application-process/cover-letters), [TARGETjobs](http://targetjobs.co.uk/careers-advice/cvs-applications-and-tests/covering-letter-essentials-graduate-vacancies), [MIT CAPD](https://capd.mit.edu/resources/how-to-write-an-effective-cover-letter/)). LSE wants a hook that goes beyond your degree title, such as *why* you chose it or your focus within it.
- John Lees, quoted in HBR (Amy Gallo, 2014), calls a bare "I'm applying for X" opening wasted space and advises against humour in the opener ([HBR](https://hbr.org/2014/02/how-to-write-a-cover-letter), via [secondary summary](https://www.computerworld.com/article/1391527/cheat-sheet-how-to-write-a-cover-letter.html)). Green advises opening with genuine, specific interest rather than boilerplate ([Ask a Manager](https://www.askamanager.org/2018/05/how-to-write-a-great-cover-letter.html)).
- **Disagreement / UK nuance**: UK services (Prospects, TARGETjobs, Glasgow, Surrey) still expect the role and where you saw the advert in the first paragraph. US experts (Green, Lees) push harder for a hook-first opener. **Resolution for UK students: one sentence that both names the role and carries a hook.** Don't open with a dramatic anecdote, but don't open with a bare administrative line either.
- Clichés to ban (consensus from UCSB, TARGETjobs, Lees): "I am writing to apply for…", "dream job", "fits my values perfectly", "Please find attached my CV" as the whole letter, "To whom it may concern" ([UCSB](https://career.ucsb.edu/get-hired/cover-letters/polish-the-writing), [TARGETjobs](http://targetjobs.co.uk/careers-advice/cvs-applications-and-tests/covering-letter-essentials-graduate-vacancies), [GMU](https://writingcenter.gmu.edu/guides/writing-cover-letters)).

### 2.7 Length: one page. About 250–400 words is the defensible target. — STRONG (one page) / WEAK (exact word count)
- One page is universal: [Oxford](https://www.ox.ac.uk/careers/job-search-and-applications/writing-applications/cover-letters), [LSE](http://info.lse.ac.uk/current-students/careers/information-and-resources/application-process/cover-letters), [Prospects](https://www.prospects.ac.uk/careers-advice/cvs-and-cover-letters/cover-letters) ("shorter is better"), [TARGETjobs](http://targetjobs.co.uk/careers-advice/cvs-applications-and-tests/covering-letter-essentials-graduate-vacancies), [MIT](https://capd.mit.edu/resources/how-to-write-an-effective-cover-letter/), [Harvard OCS](https://careerservices.fas.harvard.edu/resources/create-a-strong-resume/), [GMU](https://writingcenter.gmu.edu/guides/writing-cover-letters).
- No cheating to fit: don't shrink the font or margins ([LSE](http://info.lse.ac.uk/current-students/careers/information-and-resources/application-process/cover-letters); Gallo in [HBR](https://hbr.org/2014/02/how-to-write-a-cover-letter)).
- Word counts: the widely repeated 250–400 range traces mostly to content-marketing blogs. The often-cited ResumeGo cover letter figures are inconsistent and unverified ([search notes](https://coverlettercopilot.ai/blog/how-many-words-should-a-cover-letter-be)). Treat it as convention, not evidence. It is still the right *target* because one A4 page at 11pt with a header fits roughly 350–450 body words.

### 2.8 Tone and register for students: formal, plain, confident, not boastful. — STRONG
- Formal and professional, with no jokes, slang, sarcasm, or emoticons ([Prospects](https://www.prospects.ac.uk/careers-advice/cvs-and-cover-letters/cover-letters), [TARGETjobs](http://targetjobs.co.uk/careers-advice/cvs-applications-and-tests/covering-letter-essentials-graduate-vacancies), [QUB](https://www.qub.ac.uk/sites/graduate-support/FileStore/Filetoupload,971827,en.pdf), [Oxford](https://www.ox.ac.uk/careers/job-search-and-applications/writing-applications/cover-letters)).
- Plain language and short sentences. Don't try to impress with obscure words ([Surrey](https://blogs.surrey.ac.uk/careers/2017/11/23/covering-letter-tips-for-students-and-recent-graduates/), [LSE](http://info.lse.ac.uk/current-students/careers/information-and-resources/application-process/cover-letters)). Explain institution-specific jargon such as "prelims" ([Oxford](https://www.ox.ac.uk/careers/job-search-and-applications/writing-applications/cover-letters)).
- LSE's specific bans: hedges ("I feel that", "I believe I could"), emotive words ("honoured", "love"), vague quantifiers ("various", "some", "many"), and buzzwords. Use active voice and quantify ([LSE](http://info.lse.ac.uk/current-students/careers/information-and-resources/application-process/cover-letters)).
- UCSB: vary sentence openings and don't start many sentences with "I". Aim for "professional, not robotic" ([UCSB](https://career.ucsb.edu/get-hired/cover-letters/polish-the-writing)). Harvard: use "I" sparingly and avoid flowery language ([Harvard OCS](https://careerservices.fas.harvard.edu/resources/create-a-strong-resume/)).
- Focus on what you contribute, not what you would gain ([Prospects](https://www.prospects.ac.uk/careers-advice/cvs-and-cover-letters/cover-letters), [GMU](https://writingcenter.gmu.edu/guides/writing-cover-letters)).
- **Disagreement on personality**: Green, GMU, and Bright Network favour letting your voice show ("sounds like you"). UK university services lean formal. Resolution: personality comes through *specific content* (what you chose, why, what you noticed), not through informal register.

### 2.9 Salutation and sign-off. — MODERATE (UK conventions STRONG, fallback greeting contested)
- Find a name first. Check the advert, LinkedIn, or the firm's site, or phone the organisation ([Prospects](https://www.prospects.ac.uk/careers-advice/cvs-and-cover-letters/cover-letters), [TARGETjobs](http://targetjobs.co.uk/careers-advice/cvs-applications-and-tests/covering-letter-essentials-graduate-vacancies), [GMU](https://writingcenter.gmu.edu/guides/writing-cover-letters)).
- Fallbacks disagree:
  - "Dear Hiring Manager": MIT ([MIT CAPD](https://capd.mit.edu/resources/how-to-write-an-effective-cover-letter/)), Prospects (only after you've exhausted other options) ([Prospects](https://www.prospects.ac.uk/careers-advice/cvs-and-cover-letters/cover-letters)). Green's published sample letters use it ([Ask a Manager sample](https://www.askamanager.org/2013/11/this-is-a-resume-and-cover-letter-that-work.html)).
  - "Dear Sir/Madam": LSE and TARGETjobs, both UK and traditional ([LSE](http://info.lse.ac.uk/current-students/careers/information-and-resources/application-process/cover-letters), [TARGETjobs](http://targetjobs.co.uk/careers-advice/cvs-applications-and-tests/covering-letter-essentials-graduate-vacancies)).
  - "To whom it may concern": discouraged by GMU and most guides. A Resume Companion survey of more than 1,000 US hiring managers found most say it has little or no effect, but a minority penalise it ([AACSB summary](https://www.aacsb.edu/insights/articles/2020/05/getting-hiring-managers-attention-to-whom-it-may-concern)). Low upside, some downside, so avoid.
  - "Dear [Firm] [Division] Team" (e.g. "Dear Graduate Recruitment Team"): not explicitly endorsed by the sources found. It is a common modern convention, and for large UK graduate programmes the real reader is a recruitment team. *Recommendation, not evidence-backed*: prefer a team name when the programme names one, then "Dear Hiring Manager".
- UK sign-off: "Yours sincerely" with a named recipient, "Yours faithfully" with "Dear Sir/Madam" ([Prospects](https://www.prospects.ac.uk/careers-advice/cvs-and-cover-letters/cover-letters), [LSE](http://info.lse.ac.uk/current-students/careers/information-and-resources/application-process/cover-letters), [Surrey](https://blogs.surrey.ac.uk/careers/2017/11/23/covering-letter-tips-for-students-and-recent-graduates/), [TARGETjobs](http://targetjobs.co.uk/careers-advice/cvs-applications-and-tests/covering-letter-essentials-graduate-vacancies)). For "Dear Hiring Manager" or a team, the sources are silent. Prospects implies "faithfully" for any general greeting. "Yours sincerely" is increasingly accepted and rarely penalised. *Open question.* US guides prefer "Sincerely" ([USC guide](https://sc.edu/about/offices_and_divisions/career_center/documents/students_cover_letter_guidelines.pdf)), which is wrong for UK letters.

### 2.10 Handling thin or no direct experience. — STRONG
- Draw on academics, coursework, projects, societies, part-time jobs, volunteering, and competitions, and show how they transfer ([TARGETjobs](http://targetjobs.co.uk/careers-advice/cvs-applications-and-tests/covering-letter-essentials-graduate-vacancies), [Michael Page](https://michaelpage.co.uk/advice/career-advice/graduate-advice/how-write-graduate-cover-letter), [MIT CAPD](https://capd.mit.edu/resources/how-to-write-an-effective-cover-letter/), [Ask a Manager via UMN](https://crk.umn.edu/sites/crk.umn.edu/files/2023-03/cover-letters.pdf): evidence need not come from paid work).
- Frame gaps positively. Say how you are building the skill rather than apologising ([LSE](http://info.lse.ac.uk/current-students/careers/information-and-resources/application-process/cover-letters), [Prospects](https://www.prospects.ac.uk/careers-advice/cvs-and-cover-letters/cover-letters)). Never write "Although I have no experience…".
- MIT: when switching fields or light on experience, be explicit about your interest and lean on transferable skills ([MIT CAPD](https://capd.mit.edu/resources/how-to-write-an-effective-cover-letter/)).

### 2.11 Close with substance and a forward-looking call to action. — MODERATE
- Restate fit and enthusiasm with something specific, say you are available for interview, and thank the reader. Avoid generic closing phrases ([LSE](http://info.lse.ac.uk/current-students/careers/information-and-resources/application-process/cover-letters), [MIT CAPD](https://capd.mit.edu/resources/how-to-write-an-effective-cover-letter/), [TARGETjobs](http://targetjobs.co.uk/careers-advice/cvs-applications-and-tests/covering-letter-essentials-graduate-vacancies), [Glasgow](https://www.gla.ac.uk/myglasgow/careers/career-development/graduate-information/applying-for-graduate-jobs/graduate-cover-letters)).
- Prospects goes further: ask for an interview and note dates you're unavailable ([Prospects](https://www.prospects.ac.uk/careers-advice/cvs-and-cover-letters/cover-letters)). Some US guides suggest promising to follow up. That is risky for portal-based UK grad schemes, so skip it.
- Disagreement: "ask for the interview" (Prospects) vs "say you look forward to hearing" (Glasgow, TARGETjobs). Both are acceptable. The rule is: no empty filler such as "I hope to hear from you soon."

### 2.12 Formatting. — STRONG
- Business letter layout. Same font and size as the CV. 10–12pt. Do not shrink margins. PDF unless told otherwise. Sensible filename. Include the job reference ([LSE](http://info.lse.ac.uk/current-students/careers/information-and-resources/application-process/cover-letters), [Prospects](https://www.prospects.ac.uk/careers-advice/cvs-and-cover-letters/cover-letters), [MIT](https://capd.mit.edu/resources/how-to-write-an-effective-cover-letter/), [Harvard OCS](https://careerservices.fas.harvard.edu/resources/create-a-strong-resume/), [TARGETjobs](http://targetjobs.co.uk/careers-advice/cvs-applications-and-tests/covering-letter-essentials-graduate-vacancies)).
- No colours, unusual fonts, GIFs, emoticons, or abbreviations ([QUB](https://www.qub.ac.uk/sites/graduate-support/FileStore/Filetoupload,971827,en.pdf)).
- No bullet points in the body. Prose is the point, because it shows communication skill and personality that CV bullets can't ([GMU](https://writingcenter.gmu.edu/guides/writing-cover-letters); Green). *MODERATE*: some US guides tolerate a short bullet list of matched requirements, but no UK service recommends it.
- Follow the employer's instructions over any general rule ([Oxford](https://www.ox.ac.uk/careers/job-search-and-applications/writing-applications/cover-letters), [LSE](http://info.lse.ac.uk/current-students/careers/information-and-resources/application-process/cover-letters)).

### 2.13 Proofread. Errors are read as poor attention to detail. — STRONG
- Every source mentions this. TARGETjobs says recruiters read errors as weak attention to detail ([TARGETjobs](http://targetjobs.co.uk/careers-advice/cvs-applications-and-tests/covering-letter-essentials-graduate-vacancies)). The most damaging error is the **wrong company name** left over from another letter (implied by the swap test). For a UK audience, use British spelling.

## 3. Annotated weak-vs-strong examples

All examples are original and illustrative. They use a fictional student, Aisha, a second-year Economics student, applying to a fictional "Northbridge Capital" Summer Analyst programme.

### Example A — Opening paragraph

**Weak**
> I am writing to apply for the Summer Internship at your company, which I saw advertised on your website. I am a hard-working and motivated second-year student who is passionate about finance, and I believe I would be a great fit for this role.

- Opens with the banned administrative cliché (CL-ST-06).
- "your company" means the firm is not named, so it fails the swap test (CL-ST-01).
- Programme title is imprecise ("Summer Internship" vs "2027 Summer Analyst Programme, Markets").
- Three unsupported self-descriptors ("hard-working", "motivated", "passionate") (CL-ST-03).
- Hedge "I believe I would" (CL-ST-12).

**Strong**
> I am applying for Northbridge Capital's 2027 Summer Analyst Programme in Markets (ref. SA-MKT-27). Running the trading desk of UCL's student investment fund this year taught me that the hardest part of a trade is not the idea but sizing it, and Northbridge's emphasis on risk ownership from week one is the reason I want to learn that discipline with you.

- First sentence: exact programme, division, and reference. This meets the UK expectation (CL-ST-05).
- Second sentence is the hook: a specific experience plus an insight plus a firm-specific link. It could not be sent to another bank unchanged.
- 61 words, inside the 40–70 target.

### Example B — Evidence paragraph ("why me")

**Weak**
> I have strong analytical and teamwork skills, which I have developed through my degree and extracurricular activities. I am also an excellent communicator and work well under pressure. My CV shows that I have been involved in many societies and have held various positions of responsibility.

- Four claims, zero evidence (CL-ST-03).
- "various", "many" are vague quantifiers (CL-ST-12).
- Points the reader to the CV instead of adding to it (CL-ST-04).

**Strong**
> The role asks for rigorous analysis under time pressure. In the Warwick Trading Challenge, my team of four had 48 hours to build a macro thesis. I built the rates scenario model in Excel, stress-tested it against the 2022 gilt sell-off, and presented the downside case that changed our position size; we placed 3rd of 62 teams. The same pressure applies in my part-time role at a café on Saturdays, where I train new staff during the busiest shift. Explaining a process clearly to someone overwhelmed is a skill I would bring to an analyst desk that supports traders in real time.

- Names the requirement from the advert, then proves it (CL-ST-02).
- Action verbs, the student's own contribution ("I built", not "we did"), a number, and an outcome (CL-ST-08).
- A non-finance job turned into transferable evidence (CL-ST-10).
- Ends with the "so what" link forward to the role (CL-ST-09).

### Example C — "Why them" paragraph

**Weak**
> Northbridge Capital is a world-leading investment bank with a strong reputation and excellent culture. I am impressed by your commitment to innovation and diversity, and I would love the opportunity to work for such a prestigious firm.

- Every phrase could describe any bank (fails CL-ST-01).
- Emotive word ("love") and flattery ("prestigious") (CL-ST-12).
- About what the firm is, not why that matters to the applicant (CL-ST-07).

**Strong**
> At your Spring Insight Day in March, a Markets associate explained how Northbridge rotates summer analysts across both flow and structured desks before placement. Having only seen the flow side through our society fund, I want that comparison before I specialise. Your recent expansion of the sterling rates desk is also directly relevant to the gilt-market work I have been doing in my dissertation proposal.

- Two concrete, verifiable facts specific to the firm.
- Each fact is tied to the applicant's experience or goal (CL-ST-07).
- Shows real research, including a contact, not website paraphrase.
- Hard rule for the AI: any such fact **must come from the job description or user-supplied research**. It must never be invented (CL-ST-15).

### Example D — Thin experience (first-year spring week applicant)

**Weak**
> Although I don't have any professional experience yet, I am very eager to learn and I think this spring week would be a great opportunity for me to develop my skills.

- Opens with the deficit (CL-ST-10).
- Centres the applicant's gain, not the contribution (CL-ST-11).
- Hedge "I think" (CL-ST-12).

**Strong**
> My first term studying Mathematics with Economics has been where I learned how I work best: I set up a weekly problem-solving group for our Probability module that grew from three to fourteen students, and I now write the solution sheets. I would bring that habit of breaking complex problems into steps others can follow to the case exercises on your Spring Week.

- Academic and extracurricular evidence carries the paragraph.
- A concrete number (3 → 14) and an ongoing responsibility.
- Links forward to a known feature of spring weeks (case exercises).

### Example E — Closing paragraph

**Weak**
> Thank you for taking the time to read my application. I hope to hear from you soon. Please do not hesitate to contact me if you require any further information.

- All filler. It could end any letter in existence (CL-ST-13).

**Strong**
> I would bring a trading-desk habit of sizing risk before acting, and the communication skills to explain it quickly. I would welcome the chance to discuss how I could contribute to the Markets team this summer, and I am available for interview at any point after 2 December. Thank you for your consideration.

- One-sentence summary of the fit, call to action, a concrete availability date, and thanks. 52 words.

## 4. Candidate rules

| ID | Rule | Why | Severity | Machine-checkable? |
|----|------|-----|----------|--------------------|
| CL-ST-01 | The letter must name the target employer (and division or programme) and contain at least one sentence that would be false or meaningless if another employer's name were substituted. | Generic letters are the most cited failure. LSE's swap test. | critical | LLM-judge (swap test). Regex: company name present ≥ 1× in body. |
| CL-ST-02 | The body must explicitly address 2–3 requirements taken from the job description or person specification, each with its own evidence. | Requirement-to-proof mapping is the core of "why me" (LSE, GMU, HSPH). | critical | LLM-judge (extract JD requirements, check coverage). Partial keyword overlap check. |
| CL-ST-03 | Every skill or trait claim (e.g. "analytical", "team player", "passionate") must be followed in the same or next sentence by a concrete example, or else removed. | "Show, don't tell". Unsupported claim lists are the weakest pattern (GMU, LSE, UCSB). | critical | LLM-judge. Regex flags trait adjectives for review. |
| CL-ST-04 | Do not restate CV bullets. Each example used must add context (how, why, what you learned, what changed) that the CV line does not contain. Never refer the reader to "my CV shows…" as a substitute for evidence. | Most common mistake per Green, LSE, TARGETjobs. | major | LLM-judge (compare with CV text). n-gram overlap with CV > threshold is a flag. |
| CL-ST-05 | The first paragraph must state the exact role or programme title (plus the reference number if the advert gives one) and include one specific hook linking the applicant to this role or firm. | UK convention needs the role up front. Experts say the first line must earn attention. | major | Regex (role title present in first paragraph). LLM-judge (hook specificity). |
| CL-ST-06 | Ban cliché openers and stock phrases: "I am writing to apply/express my interest", "To whom it may concern", "dream job", "perfect fit", "fits my values perfectly", "Please find attached", "I would like to take this opportunity". | Wasted opening space and immediate generic signal (Lees/HBR, UCSB, TARGETjobs, GMU). | major | Regex (blocklist). |
| CL-ST-07 | The "why them" content must cite at least one employer-specific fact (business area, programme feature, deal, product, event, or person met) and connect it to the applicant's own experience or goal. | Generic praise is a top mistake. Motivation must be personal (LSE, TARGETjobs, Glasgow). | critical | LLM-judge. |
| CL-ST-08 | Evidence sentences use active voice, first-person singular action verbs for the applicant's own contribution ("I built", not "we were involved in"), and include a number or a concrete outcome where the source data has one. | Quantification and active voice are recommended by LSE and TARGETjobs. "I did", not "I would" (WSU). | major | Partial regex (passive-voice detector, digit presence per evidence paragraph). LLM-judge. |
| CL-ST-09 | Each evidence example ends by linking forward to how it applies in the target role (the "so what"). | STARS "So what?" (UCSB). MIT: connect skills to the job. | major | LLM-judge. |
| CL-ST-10 | Never open a sentence or paragraph with a deficit ("Although I have no experience…", "Despite lacking…"). Frame gaps through transferable evidence or how the skill is being built. | LSE and Prospects: frame gaps positively. MIT: transferable skills. | major | Regex ("no (prior\|previous\|professional) experience", "although I (don't\|do not) have", "lack"). |
| CL-ST-11 | Weight the letter towards what the applicant will contribute, not what they will gain. "Opportunity to develop/learn/grow" phrasing may appear at most once. | Prospects and GMU: contribution over gain. | minor | Regex count ("opportunity to (learn\|develop\|grow)"). LLM-judge for overall balance. |
| CL-ST-12 | Ban hedges ("I feel that", "I believe I could", "I think"), vague quantifiers ("various", "many", "some", "numerous"), and emotive or flattering words ("honoured", "love", "passionate", "prestigious", "world-leading") unless they are directly evidenced. | LSE explicit list. UCSB: sweeping claims. | minor | Regex (blocklist with counts). |
| CL-ST-13 | The closing paragraph must contain a one-sentence fit summary and a forward call to action (interest in interview, availability) in 25–50 words, and must not consist only of stock lines ("I hope to hear from you soon", "Do not hesitate to contact me"). | LSE: avoid generic closes. MIT, Prospects: restate fit and request interview. | minor | Length check plus regex blocklist. LLM-judge for the fit summary. |
| CL-ST-14 | Body length 250–400 words (hard max 450), 4–5 paragraphs, each paragraph ≤ about 110 words. Must fit one A4 page at 10–12pt without shrinking margins. If the employer specifies a limit, that limit overrides. | One page is universal. Short paragraphs suit skimming (Glasgow). Word range is convention. | major | Length (word, paragraph, and character counts). Render check for one page. |
| CL-ST-15 | Every factual claim about the applicant must trace to the CV or profile, and every claim about the employer must trace to the job description or user-supplied research. Never invent numbers, events, contacts, or firm facts. | Fabrication is an integrity failure that interviews expose. Specificity tempts the model to hallucinate. | critical | LLM-judge (grounding check against inputs). |
| CL-ST-16 | Salutation: use a named person if one is supplied. Otherwise use a named team if the programme gives one (e.g. "Dear Graduate Recruitment Team"), otherwise "Dear Hiring Manager". Never use "To whom it may concern". | Name first (Prospects, TARGETjobs, GMU). "To whom it may concern" has low upside and some penalty (AACSB survey). | major | Regex. |
| CL-ST-17 | UK sign-off: "Yours sincerely" when the recipient is named. "Yours faithfully" with "Dear Sir/Madam". For a team or "Hiring Manager" greeting, default to "Yours sincerely" (configurable). Never use the US-only "Sincerely," alone for UK employers. | UK convention (Prospects, LSE, Surrey, TARGETjobs). | minor | Regex (salutation and sign-off consistency). |
| CL-ST-18 | Prose only: no bullet lists, headings, emoji, colour, or decorative formatting in the letter body. Use British spelling. | Prose shows communication skill (GMU, Green). Formatting bans (QUB). UK audience. | minor | Regex (bullets, emoji). Spell-check against an en-GB dictionary. |
| CL-ST-19 | Vary sentence openings: no more than about 35% of sentences start with "I", and no two consecutive paragraphs start with "I". | "Use 'I' sparingly" (Harvard). Vary openers (UCSB). | minor | Regex or count. |
| CL-ST-20 | Plain register: average sentence length ≤ about 22 words, no sentence > 40 words, no unexplained abbreviations or institution-specific jargon. | Short, simple sentences (TARGETjobs, LSE, Surrey). Oxford: explain jargon. | minor | Length (sentence stats). Abbreviation regex. |
| CL-ST-21 | Order the middle sections so the applicant's stronger material comes first: evidence-led ("why me" first) when they have direct experience, motivation-led ("why them" first) when experience is thin but research is strong. | Sources allow either order. Lead with strength. | minor | No (generation policy). |
| CL-ST-22 | The letter must contain no other employer's name and no placeholder text ("[Company]", "XYZ", "Lorem"). | A wrong-company slip is fatal and common with reuse. | critical | Regex (bracket placeholders, known-employer list minus target). |

## 5. Open questions

1. **Fallback salutation in UK grad recruitment.** Is "Dear Graduate Recruitment Team" actually preferred over "Dear Hiring Manager" by UK recruiters? No source found tests this. "Dear Sir/Madam" is still recommended by LSE and TARGETjobs but reads dated. A recruiter survey or the employer-specific researcher (another angle) may resolve it.
2. **Sign-off after "Dear Hiring Manager".** Strictly, "faithfully" (unnamed) or "sincerely"? UK sources are silent. Most UK readers won't penalise either. Should Placed make it configurable?
3. **Exact word-count evidence.** The 250–400 word figure is convention. The ResumeGo cover-letter stats circulating online could not be verified to a primary source. Is there real A/B or callback data?
4. **Hook strength vs UK formality.** How far should the AI push a hook-first opener (Lees and Green style) for conservative sectors (banking, law, civil service) vs creative or tech roles? This probably needs a sector-tone parameter.
5. **Text-box "cover letters" in ATS portals.** Many UK grad schemes replace the letter with a 200–300-word motivation box or specific questions. Should Placed treat these as a separate mode with compressed structure?
6. **Does a separate cover letter get read at all?** HBR (Gallo) cites a figure of roughly half being read. Green and the UK services insist it matters for close cases. The rule set assumes it is read. Another researcher should quantify how often this holds for spring weeks and grad schemes.
7. **AI-detection and "voice".** Imperial's researcher course now covers the pros and cons of generative AI for cover letters. Do recruiters penalise "AI-sounding" letters, and what surface features trigger that? This is relevant to CL-ST-06, CL-ST-12, and CL-ST-19.
